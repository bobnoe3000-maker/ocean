# The Hearth — World

This file is the world of *Hearthfall*. Part One is the one page a new player reads before play, verbatim. Part Two is the depth behind it. Every heading in Part Two names the line of the one page it grows from, so nothing here exists that a player could not already have read in five minutes.

## Part One — The one page

> **THE HEARTH**
>
> The Hearth is a bowl. Draw a ring of mountains and call it the Rim. Put a lake at the bottom of the bowl and call it the Still. Cut one notch in the ring, at the south, and call it the Fall: the only way water gets out, and the only way anyone gets in. Three rivers run down from the Rim to the Still: the Halse from the north, the Twine from the east, the Rudd from the west. Between the mountains and the lake the land lies in rings: the Shoulders (high moor, and the standing stones), the Wend (the forest belt), the Lowmark (farms, the Ring Road, the four towns) and the Shelf (the old lakebed, mud and salt, wider every year, because the Still is still falling).
>
> Four peoples live here. The **Ulder** were first: tall, grey-eyed, long-lived, few. They keep the stones and teach Drawing. The **Vael** farm the Lowmark and keep the towns; they are most of everybody, and you are one of them. The **Skerrow** hold the Rim: miners and herders who cut the Fall and never let anyone forget it. The **Kest** came last, up the Ebb from the outer sea ninety-one years ago. They built the Stair, and they own the ledgers.
>
> Three ages, each remembered for one thing. The **Age of Stone**: the Ulder raised their courts on the floor of the bowl, and then the water rose and drowned them. The **Age of the Cutting**: the Skerrow cut the Fall, the Still fell, and the Vael farmed the mud it left. The **Age of Ships**: the Kest came up the Fall, built the Stair, and coin came into the Hearth.
>
> Five powers want things. The **Moot**, the Vael towns' council at Brenning, wants the roads free of tolls and the harvest in. The **Stair**, the Kest company at Fallgate, wants every debt in the Hearth written in its book. The **Holds**, the Skerrow mine-halls under the Rim, want the Wend's timber and whatever bronze the Shelf gives up. The **Quiet**, the Ulder who keep the stones, want the Shelf left alone; some of them want the Fall closed and the water back. The **Wrack** — debtors, deserters and the Salted, out on the Shelf under Halse Corrow, who was Reeve of Wickery once — wants no lord, no ledger, and the towns afraid.
>
> Magic is **Drawing**. Water remembers, the Ulder say, and a Drawer pulls on it. Every working takes water from something: the nearest puddle, a well, a body — and when nothing else is near, from the Drawer. The price is thirst. The ledger is salt: draw too deep and salt crusts in you and never leaves. Draw deep enough and you are Salted, and you walk the Shelf with the Wrack.
>
> The year has four seasons — Thaw, Green, Reap, Hold — and a festival in each: Firstwater, when the ice goes off the Still; Longsun, the fair; the Reckoning, when debts come due and the low-mark is cut into the quay post; and Stillnight, when nobody travels.
>
> You are a villager of Sallowford on the Rudd, with a trade and no destiny. What you become, the Hearth will decide — by who knows your name.

*(474 words. Read aloud at a plain pace it takes four and a half minutes.)*

## Part Two — The depth

### 2.1 "The Hearth is a bowl" — geography

The map has one shape and it is drawn like this: a circle for the Rim; a dot in the middle for the Still; a notch at the bottom for the Fall; three lines from the rim to the dot for the rivers (top, right, left); a ring road drawn about two-thirds of the way in, touching four towns at the compass points. Anyone who has seen it once can draw it on a table with a finger.

**The Rim.** A ring of mountains eighty to a hundred miles across, unbroken except at the Fall. Three passes are used: Halse Pass (north), Twine Pass (east) and Rudd's Head (west), each held by a Skerrow hold cut into the rock below it: Kell, Orrit and Thrum. Beyond the passes is more stone and then snow; the Skerrow say nobody has walked to the far side and come back with anything worth carrying. The Rim is the edge of the known world for everyone in the bowl except the Kest, who came from outside it.

**The Shoulders.** The high moor below the peaks: heather, tarns, sheep, wind, and the standing stones of the Ulder in rings of nine. This was the Ulder's shore in the Age of Stone, when the water stood this high, and the old water-line is still a visible step in the ground that the Vael call the Old Shore. Few people live here: Ulder at the stones, Skerrow shepherds in summer.

**The Wend.** The forest belt below the Shoulders, a day's walk deep in most places. Oak and alder on the lower slopes, pine higher. The Ulder live here in the numbers they have left. The Holds want its timber for pit-props; the Quiet fell nothing. Roads through the Wend follow the rivers; off the roads it is Ulder ground and everyone knows it.

**The Lowmark.** The farmed ring: the mud the Cutting left, six hundred years of ploughing later. Wheat, barley, flax, cattle, orchards near the towns. The **Ring Road** runs round it, roughly where the Lowmark meets the Shelf, linking the four towns: **Brenning** (north, on the Halse, the Moot's seat), **Tarrow** (east, on the Twine, the iron market), **Wickery** (west, on the Rudd, the fishing town and the Shelf's edge) and **Fallgate** (south, at the head of the Stair, Kest-built). Three spoke roads leave the Ring Road along the rivers up to the passes. Every village in the Hearth is on a river, a spoke road or the Ring Road; there is nowhere else to be.

**The Shelf.** Below the Ring Road the ground drops a man's height at the Present Shore and becomes the Shelf: cracked mud, salt pans, reeds at the water's edge, and, sticking out of the silt, the courts of the Age of Stone as the Still uncovers them. The Shelf is widest in the west and south because the bowl is shallowest there; at Wickery it is four miles from the town to the water and was two when the town was founded. The Shelf is Wrack ground, and nobody's ground, and the ground the Holds and the Stair most want.

**The Still.** The lake. Fresh, cold, deep in the middle (the Ulder say bottomless; the Kest say four hundred fathoms; nobody has proved either). It drains through one river, **the Ebb**, which leaves the bowl at the Fall.

**The Fall.** The notch in the southern Rim: a gorge a mile long and a quarter-mile deep where the Ebb goes out in a stair of rapids and falls to the outer sea. It is not one waterfall; it is the whole broken slope. Down its eastern wall the Kest built **the Stair**, a road of switchbacks, winches and lock-basins by which goods (not ships) go up and down. At the top is Fallgate; at the bottom, outside the Hearth, the Kest port called the Foot. Everything that enters or leaves the Hearth goes through the Fall, and the Stair Company owns the Stair.

**Why things are where they are.** Towns sit where rivers meet the Shelf because that is where boats met carts. The Ring Road follows the Present Shore because the Lowmark above it is too valuable to pave and the Shelf below it too soft. The passes are where the Skerrow found ore. The stones are on the Shoulders because that was the shore when they were raised. The Wrack is on the Shelf because the Shelf is where the ruins are and nobody can hold it.

**Scale.** Wickery to Brenning by the Ring Road is about forty miles: two days' walk. Thrum to Wickery down the Rudd is thirty miles. The whole Ring Road is about a hundred and sixty miles. The first region, the Rudd valley, is described in [FIRST_REGION](../slice/FIRST_REGION.md).

### 2.2 "Three ages, each remembered for one thing" — history and what it left behind

Dates are counted two ways: the Vael and Moot count from the Cutting (the present year is **611 C.**); the Kest and Stair count from the Stair's first winch (**91 S.**). The Ulder do not count; they remember.

**The Age of Stone (before about 1,500 years ago).** The Ulder alone in the bowl, which was then mostly dry: the Still was a small lake and the Ulder built their courts — rings of halls around a central well, in stone and the copper-gold alloy the Vael now call stone-bronze — on the bowl's floor, and raised the standing stones on the heights above to hold the weather and ward the courts. The age ended in the **Drowning**: the water rose, over a lifetime, until it stood at the Shoulders. The Ulder retreated to the heights, and the courts went under. The Ulder say they called the water themselves; the Vael say the sky did it; no Ulder alive was there, and the ones who keep the stones do not discuss it.

*What it left:* the standing stones, which still work (a stone ring turns weather and eases salt; see 2.6); the drowned courts, now surfacing on the Shelf one by one, each a dungeon with stone-bronze in it and Salted things in its lower halls; Drawing, which the Ulder taught the shore-folk who became the Vael; the Ulder's grief, which is the Quiet's whole agenda.

**The Age of the Cutting (about 600 years ago).** The Skerrow, mining the Rim from the outside-in, broke through into the bowl at the southern notch while chasing an ore seam, found the lake pressing against their galleries, and cut the notch open on purpose to save the mine. The Still fell for a generation. What it left was mud, and the shore-folk — the Vael, who had lived on the Shoulders fishing the high lake under the Ulder's teaching — walked down onto it and farmed. In two hundred years there were towns; in three, the Moot. The Ulder never came down.

*What it left:* the Fall; the Lowmark and everything on it; the Ring Road; the Moot; the split between Vael and Ulder (teachers and pupils, now landlords and hermits); and the Skerrow's standing claim that the Hearth's every field is a gift of theirs, which they put in every contract's preamble.

**The Age of Ships (91 years ago to now).** A Kest survey ship put into the gorge's foot looking for a harbour and found a river coming out of a mountain. Within ten years the Kest had the Stair built; within thirty, Fallgate; within sixty, a counting-house in every town; and now, in 91 S., the Stair Company holds the debt of about a third of the Lowmark's farms and the whole Moot treasury. Coin came in with the Kest. Before them the Hearth traded by tally-stick and standing debt; the Kest brought silver marks and copper bits and a ledger for everyone.

*What it left:* the Stair, the only door; the Chain (the Company's hired blades); the debt; the Wrack, which is made of people the debt broke; and the present, in which you begin.

**Why the history still matters in play.** Every dungeon is Age of Stone. Every land dispute is Age of the Cutting (the Holds' preamble, the Quiet's grievance). Every quest with money in it is Age of Ships. The engine's lore tables are tagged by age, and a quest that draws a place, an opposition and a motive from three different ages is one that has the whole history in it (see [ADVENTURE_ENGINE](../systems/ADVENTURE_ENGINE.md), "Drawing from the lore").

### 2.3 "Four peoples live here" — the peoples

Numbers are the Moot's 601 C. head-count plus the Stair's 90 S. survey; they disagree by about a tenth and the Ulder were never counted.

**The Vael** (about 60,000). Farmers, fishers, millers, smiths, carters, reeves. Plain-spoken, tallying, superstitious about hearths (a Vael house never lets its fire go fully out; a cold hearth is a house given up). They swear "by my hearth". They keep memory in ledgers and songs and expect debts to be paid, which is why the Stair found them such good customers. Names: a given name and a byname from trade, place or parent — *Wenna Cooper, Bram Fenwright, Idony Sallow, Osk, Hild Marrow, Reyne Gorse, Aud Penhallow, Jory Quill, Merrin Hale, Dell Coombe, Tobbin*. Given names are one or two syllables; bynames are plain words. Nobody has a title except reeves, and a reeve is a neighbour who was chosen.

**The Skerrow** (about 9,000). Rim folk of the three holds and the summer moors. Broad, short, weathered, loud. Contract-keepers: a Skerrow bargain is spoken aloud in front of a witness and then it is stone, and they treat breaking one as a kind of murder. They distrust Drawing (water in a mine is the enemy) and love iron, timber, and being owed. Names: hard, doubled consonants, then the hold — *Brakka of Thrum, Hemmet Dagg, Marrock of Thrum, Gulla, Rukk, Torv, Skell, Ott*. A Skerrow away from the Rim always says which hold they are from, first.

**The Ulder** (perhaps 2,000; nobody knows). Tall, grey-eyed, slow-spoken, living three hundred years, bearing few children. They live in the Wend and at the stones, and they are the only people who can teach Drawing properly; every Vael herb-wife who dowses learned it from an Ulder or from someone who did. They are not wise, exactly — they are old, and remember things the way a river does, in a channel. Their grievance against everyone below the Old Shore is not hatred; it is grief with a very long half-life. Names: three syllables, stress on the first, and instead of a byname the stone ring they keep — *Sarane of the Rudd Stones, Ondrin, Ilune, Tessarin, Reval, Coreth, Ushe of the Halse Stones*.

**The Kest** (about 4,000 in the Hearth; more at the Foot and beyond). Sea-people from the outer coast, dark-haired, well-dressed, fast-talking, who worship nothing but keep weights and measures the way others keep gods ("what can be weighed can be owned"). They write the family name first. They are not villains; they are the only people in the Hearth who have seen the outside, and they think the Hearth is a marvel that badly needs a proper ledger. Names: family then given — *Vesk Corradine, Vesk Idren, Anser Sabel, Drusk Fennick, Pellow Orsa, Marl Tamsin*. The Company is family business; most senior Company people are Vesks or married to one.

**How the peoples fit.** Ulder taught Vael; Skerrow drained Ulder's water and gave Vael their land; Kest bought Vael's land back with debt and pay Skerrow for iron; Ulder want the water back, which would drown Vael, Kest and the Holds' lower galleries alike. There is no side without a reason and no reason without a cost.

### 2.4 "Five powers want things" — the powers and their agendas

Each power is a real actor in the simulation ([LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md), "Faction simulation"): it has a seat, a leader, resources, three ranked goals and a move list. Here is what they are and want in the fiction; the numbers are in the systems file.

**The Moot.** The council of the Vael towns, seated at Brenning under **High Reeve Marrow Tull**, a wheat-farmer's son who has held the seat nine years by never quite deciding anything. Each town has a reeve; each village a headman or headwoman; the Moot levies militia by the town and taxes by the field. Goals, ranked: keep the Ring Road free of Stair toll-houses; put down the Wrack before it takes a town; keep the Holds' iron coming without ceding the Wend. Resources: militia (many but seasonal — you cannot levy at harvest), grain, the loyalty of most Vael, and no money at all: the Moot treasury is a Stair ledger.

**The Stair.** The Kest Stair Company, seated at Fallgate under **Weighmaster Vesk Corradine**, a woman of sixty who has never raised her voice and has never lost a debt. A factor in every town keeps a counting-house and a chain of six to twelve hired blades. Goals: hold every town's debt by the Reckoning of 92 S.; buy the salvage rights to the Shelf from whoever can be made to seem to own them; build a toll-house on the Ring Road, which the Moot has refused for forty years. Resources: coin (nearly all of it), the Chain, the Stair itself (they can close the door), and the law of contract, which the Skerrow respect and the Vael signed.

**The Holds.** The three Skerrow mine-halls — Thrum, Kell, Orrit — which vote as one when they can agree, under whichever hold-mother or hold-father the other two least dislike this year (at present **Hold-mother Brakka of Thrum**). Goals: timber rights in the Wend (their galleries are running out of props); the Shelf's stone-bronze, which they can work and the Vael cannot; a fixed price for iron at the Stair. Resources: iron, hewers (who fight well), the passes, contracts nobody else can break, and very few numbers.

**The Quiet.** The Ulder who keep the stones, which is most Ulder; they have no seat and no leader, only Speakers for each ring, of whom **Sarane of the Rudd Stones** is the one the first region meets. Goals: the Wend unfelled; the courts on the Shelf unrobbed; and — this is the split — the Fall closed. The **Patient** believe the water will come back in its own time and the stones must simply be kept until then. The **Closers** believe the Fall can be closed by Drawing, at a price that would Salt every Drawer who took part, and that this is the price. Resources: Drawing, the stones (which turn weather and can be made to hold or loose a river's flow), the Wend, three hundred years of memory each, and almost no one.

**The Wrack.** What the lake left behind: farmers whose debts the Stair called, militia who deserted the Moot's unpaid levies, Skerrow who broke a contract and could not go home, and the Salted — Drawers who went too deep, and older things from the courts that walk when the mud dries. They hold the western Shelf from a camp called Corrow's Reach and the surfaced court beyond it, under **Halse Corrow**, who was Reeve of Wickery until 606 C., when the Stair called Wickery's debt and he walked out onto the Shelf rather than sign, and came back half-Salted and with followers. Goals: burn ledgers (every raid ends at a counting-house); take Wickery by the Reckoning; hold the Shelf against the Holds and the Stair. Resources: numbers (every called debt sends them a recruit), the ruins, the Salted, and the towns' fear. The Wrack is not a horde; it is a grievance with knives, and about a quarter of its members have kin in Sallowford or Wickery.

**How the powers clash.** Moot against Stair over tolls and debt; Stair against Wrack over ledgers; Holds against Quiet over the Wend; Holds and Stair against Quiet and Wrack over the Shelf; Moot against Wrack over the towns; and everyone, quietly, against the Closers, who would end the argument by drowning it. A player who remembers "Moot roads, Stair debt, Holds timber, Quiet water, Wrack ledgers" has all five.

### 2.5 "Magic is Drawing" — magic and its price

Drawing is the Ulder's teaching that water remembers where it has been and what it has touched, and that a mind trained to it can pull on that memory. A Drawer can pull water to a place (a pool on dry stone, an ice-slick, a fog), pull water out of a place (dry a flooded passage, drain a puddle to read what walked through it), and pull it out of a body, which kills. That last is the working called **Wring**, and the Quiet forbid it on anything but the Salted.

**The price is water.** Every working takes water from the nearest source: a puddle, a well, rain, the mist off the Still, a waterskin, a body. A Drawer carries water for this reason — a skin at the hip is the mage's staff — and when the skin is dry and there is nothing near, the working takes it from the Drawer's own body. That is thirst: real, damaging thirst.

**The ledger is salt.** Water drawn from a body leaves its salt behind. Draw from yourself and the salt stays in you: a grey crust at the fingertips first, then the wrists, the lips, the eyes. It never leaves entirely; the stones can ease it a little, once a season. A Drawer with too much salt in them is **Salted**: stiff, dry, grey, awake, and no longer entirely themselves. The Salted things in the drowned courts are, the Ulder say, the Drawers of the Age of Stone who tried to hold back the Drowning. Halse Corrow is halfway there and knows it.

**What magic is not.** There is no fire from the hands, no flight, no raising the dead, no talking to gods (there are none named; the Vael honour the hearth, the Ulder the water, the Kest the scale). Magic is understated, physical, and priced; a Drawer in a dry place is a person with a knife and a waterskin. The mechanics — the water pool, the thirst rule, salt as a permanent scar — are specified in [CLASSES_AND_STANDING](../systems/CLASSES_AND_STANDING.md) and [COMBAT](../systems/COMBAT.md).

### 2.6 "The Ulder … keep the stones" — the stones

A stone ring is nine standing stones on the Shoulders, each about twice a man's height, raised in the Age of Stone. A kept ring (one with an Ulder keeper) does three things the simulation uses: it turns weather (a kept ring's valley gets one fewer storm per season; an unkept ring's gets one more); it eases salt (a Drawer who sleeps a night in a kept ring loses 25 Salt, once per season); and it holds or looses the river it stands above (the Closers' plan is to make every ring on the southern Rim hold at once and let the Still rise back over the Fall). The Rudd Stones stand above the Rudd's headwaters, above Thrum, and are kept by Sarane and Ondrin.

### 2.7 "The year has four seasons" — calendar and seasons

A year is 360 days: four seasons of 90 days, each of three months of 30 (First, Mid, Last — "Mid Thaw", "Last Reap"). A week is six days: five working days and **Stillday**, on which nobody trades, militia drill, and the Ulder do not speak to strangers. Day, dusk and night matter: shops keep hours, roads are safer by day, the Shelf is walked by Salted things after dark.

| Season | Weather | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Thaw** (days 1–90) | Ice off the Still on day 1; mud; rain in Mid Thaw; the Rudd runs high | Roads slow (Wend and Shelf at half speed until Mid Thaw); boats launch; the Stair's Quarter-day on day 1 (interest due); Ulder walk down to bless wells; Wrack recruit from called debts |
| **Green** (91–180) | Warm, long days, dry by Last Green | Hiring at Longsun (day 135); everyone is in the fields (militia hard to levy); the Shelf dries and the Salted walk further; Holds send hewers to the Wend |
| **Reap** (181–270) | Harvest, then storms | The Reckoning (day 240): debts due, taxes to the Moot, the low-mark cut at every quay; a called debt after Reckoning makes a Wrack recruit; the best time for war (grain in, hands free) |
| **Hold** (271–360) | Snow on the Shoulders; the Wend impassable off-road; the Still freezes at the edges | Passes closed from Mid Hold; Stillnight (day 360) — nobody travels, the Ulder walk the rings, the Salted walk the Shelf; the Wrack are hungriest |

Festivals: **Firstwater** (Thaw 1): the ice goes; boats are launched; an Ulder blesses each village well; the Stair collects its Quarter-day. **Longsun** (Green 45): the fair at each town; contests (the Fighter's bout, the Rogue's foot-race, the Mage's dowsing); hiring; Skerrow come down. **The Reckoning** (Reap 60): debts and taxes; the low-mark; the Moot sits; the Wrack raids. **Stillnight** (Hold 90): one night when the whole simulation stops moving on the roads and everything that is going to happen at the stones and on the Shelf happens.

The game begins at dawn on Firstwater, 611 C. / 91 S.

### 2.8 "You are a villager of Sallowford on the Rudd" — the first region in all of this

Sallowford is a ford-village of thirty-one souls on the Rudd, eight miles up the spoke road from Wickery, at the seam between Lowmark fields and the lower Wend. Upstream are Fenn's Mill and then the Wend, the Hewers' Camp, and at the top of the valley Thrum under Rudd's Head, with the Rudd Stones on the Shoulder above it. Downstream is Wickery, the western Shelf, the Wrack's camp at Corrow's Reach and the surfaced court the Vael call the Sunk Court. The whole valley is a cross-section of the one page: Rim to Still, Ulder to Wrack, stones to salt, in a day's walk. It is the region because a player who has walked it has walked every ring of the bowl and met every people and all five powers (the Moot through Wickery's reeve, the Stair through its factor, the Holds through Thrum, the Quiet through the Rudd Stones, and the Wrack on the Shelf; the Stair's own seat at Fallgate is region two).

Sketch of the campaign's other regions, for shape only: the Halse valley (Brenning and the Moot; Kell; the Halse Stones — where the Closers are strongest); the Twine valley (Tarrow's iron market; Orrit; the eastern Shelf's larger courts); the Fall (Fallgate, the Stair, the Foot outside); and the Still itself (boats, the deep courts, the argument's end). The campaign's map-changing end is whether the Fall stays open, and who holds the door.

### 2.9 What is deliberately not in the world

No gods with names. No other continents (the Kest's coast is "outside" and stays out of scope). No dragons, undead beyond the Salted, or beast-peoples. No language beyond a few words (Ulder "keep", Skerrow "cut", Kest "weigh"). No fifth people, sixth power or fourth age, ever: an expansion adds a valley, a court or a ship, never a new kind of thing. Depth in the Hearth is how far down a court goes, how far back a grudge goes, and how far a name travels.
