# Critic — Round 1

Judged against `rpg_design_prompt.txt`. I read PITCH.md, world/WORLD.md, the six system files,
slice/FIRST_REGION.md, docs/DECISIONS.md and docs/STATUS.json in full. I played the documents on
paper as three sessions, audited the twelve quests, tried to generate a thirteenth and fourteenth
from the stated grammar, and traced the standing road. Every claim below cites a file and a section.

**Verdict up front: this round does not pass.** Not one line reaches 8.5, and there is one
design-smell hit. That is normal for a first round and it should not be read as a poor bible — this
is a genuinely strong first draft with a world I would greenlight and a standing system I have not
seen done this well elsewhere. It fails on a single structural fact repeated in six places: **the
bible counts its tables instead of writing them, and traces its clock instead of running it.**

## 1. The friend test

### 1.1 What I would tell a friend, from Part One only

*(Read `world/WORLD.md` §"Part One — The one page" and nothing else.)*

"It's a world in a bowl. Draw a ring of mountains — that's the Rim. Put a lake at the bottom — the
Still. Cut one notch in the ring at the south — that's the Fall, the only place water gets out and
the only way anyone gets in. Three rivers run down to the lake. Between the mountains and the water
the land is in rings: high moor with standing stones, then forest, then the farmed ring with a road
round it and four towns on it, then the Shelf — the old lakebed, mud and salt, and it gets wider
every year, because the lake is still dropping.

Four peoples. The Ulder were first: tall, grey-eyed, long-lived, very few, they keep the stone
circles and they teach the magic. The Vael farm and run the towns, they're most of everybody, and
you're one. The Skerrow live in the mountains, mine, and they're the ones who cut the notch. The
Kest turned up ninety-one years ago from outside, built a road down the notch called the Stair, and
brought coin and account books.

Three ages, one line each: the Ulder's stone halls on the bowl's floor drowned when the water rose;
the Skerrow cut the notch, the water drained, and the Vael walked down and farmed the mud; the Kest
came up and wrote it all in a ledger.

Five powers, one want each. The Moot is the Vael town council: roads free of tolls. The Stair is the
Kest company: every debt in its book. The Holds are the Skerrow mines: timber and bronze. The Quiet
are the Ulder stone-keepers: leave the Shelf alone — and some of them want the notch closed and the
lake back. The Wrack are debtors and deserters out on the mud: no lords, no ledgers.

Magic is Drawing. You pull water out of whatever's nearest — a puddle, a well, a body, or yourself.
The price is thirst. The bill is salt that crusts in you and never washes out; go far enough and
you're Salted, and you go and walk on the Shelf with the outlaws.

Four seasons, a festival in each. You start a villager with a trade and no destiny, and what you
become gets decided by who ends up knowing your name."

That took me a hundred and ten seconds to say out loud, and I did not have to look anything up. The
one page works. The map is genuinely drawable from one sentence of §2.1 — circle, dot, notch, three
lines, a ring road, four towns — and I could draw it now from memory.

### 1.2 What the friend gets wrong, and whether it is the page's fault

| # | What the friend gets wrong or cannot answer | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | *"Why is the lake still falling? The Skerrow cut it six hundred years ago."* The friend assumes magic, or a leak, or an ongoing drought. | **The page's fault, and Part Two's too.** Part One says "wider every year, because the Still is still falling." §2.1, §2.7 ("the Still's low-mark drops one finger") and FIRST_REGION §5 all *use* the falling lake as the clock that surfaces the dungeons and starts the disputes, and **no file anywhere states a cause.** The single most load-bearing physical fact in the setting is unexplained at both depths. |
| **b** | *"Is 'the Stair' a road or a company?"* The friend will use it for both and be confused when the Stair "wants every debt in its book." | **The page's fault.** Part One says the Kest "built the Stair" and then lists "**The Stair**, the Kest company at Fallgate" as a power, with no disambiguating clause. §2.1 resolves it ("the Stair Company owns the Stair") but the page is the contract. This is the name of the primary antagonist power. |
| **c** | *"What do I actually fight?"* The friend will describe a political drama. | **The page's fault, mildly.** The only things on the page that could be fought are the Wrack and the implied Salted. COMBAT §1 has fifteen enemy kinds including salt-hounds, cranes, mud-eels and reedbacks. A page that a new player reads before an action RPG should carry one clause telling them something out there wants to eat them. |
| **d** | *"If the Fall is the only way in, how did the Skerrow get here?"* | **Half the page's fault.** Part One states an absolute ("the only way anyone gets in") that §2.2 immediately qualifies — the Skerrow "mining the Rim from the outside-in, broke through." Legitimate depth, but the page's absolute is too absolute. |
| **e** | *"Are the Ulder human? Why do they live so long?"* | **Correctly left out.** §2.3 gives "three hundred years, bearing few children" and no more, which is right — the Ulder work better unexplained. |
| **f** | *"If Drawing pulls water from the nearest body, don't Drawers kill people constantly?"* | **Correctly left out.** §2.5 (Wring is a deliberate working, forbidden by the Quiet on the living) and CLASSES §2 (the waterskin is the mage's staff) answer it, and the page's ominous version is better drama. |
| **g** | Cannot name the four towns; knows Brenning and Fallgate only. | **Correctly left out.** Tarrow and Wickery are depth. |
| **h** | Does not know the campaign's question (the Fall, open or closed). | **Correctly left out.** §2.8 and PITCH carry it; a player should discover it. |

**Result: the friend test passes,** with three defects on the page itself (a, b, c) and one
over-absolute clause (d). Defect (a) is not a one-page problem at all — it is a hole in the world's
physics that Part Two does not fill either.

### 2.1 The three-minute session

I refuse to re-narrate the one the bible already wrote (SESSION_UX §3, Thaw 11 — reprinted almost
verbatim as FIRST_REGION §7's check-in; **the bible contains exactly one three-minute session and
prints it twice**). I traced two others.

**Attempt one: a new player's first three minutes, Thaw 1, 06:00.**

App opens cold. Creation.
`[HOLE: the creation flow. SESSION_UX §1 counts "creation" as one of nine screens and never
describes it — no zone layout, no order, no inputs beyond CLASSES_AND_STANDING §1's "a trade and a
class", no naming, no portrait. FIRST_REGION §7 hour 0:00 says only "Creation: name, trade smith,
class Fighter." The first screen every player ever sees is unspecified.]`

Say I get through it. The Firstwater anchor at the well (FIRST_REGION §7 hour 0:00): Idony waiting
for an Ulder who does not come (her schedule, §3 row 2, is overridden by the festival), Sabel riding
through with the Stair's book (Stair opening move, §4), Reyne shouting from the pasture. Good scene.
Then hour 0:10, Tobbin behind the smithy: engine quest **V3, Tobbin's Flock** (ADVENTURE_ENGINE §7).

Now the three minutes are over, and V3 has not started. V3 is a map jump to the Old Shore road
(FIRST_REGION §1: Wickery–Sunk Court across the Shelf is 2 h, 4 h in early Thaw, and Sallowford is
3 h from Wickery), a search across a site, a first fight with a salt-hound, and a return by 15:00 —
nine world hours. The trace's own hour markers put the Firstwater anchor plus V3 at **0:00 to 0:45 —
forty-five minutes.**

`[HOLE: no three-minute session exists in the first hour of play. SESSION_UX §3 defines the
three-minute session as "the journal's top quest has a next step at one site," which presumes a
journal, a map with travelled sites, and a standing that makes people speak to you. At Thaw 1 the
player has none of the three. The bible's only two three-minute examples (SESSION_UX §3; FIRST_REGION
§7 Thaw 11 and Thaw 32) are the same session and both sit at hour 4+ of play, after Hand standing.]`

**Attempt two: an ordinary three minutes mid-campaign, Thaw 24, from the traced Fighter's state.**

The player closed at Thaw 23 19:00 (FIRST_REGION §7 hour 10:00). Six real hours away → 6 × 4 = 24
world hours (LIVING_WORLD §1, Tick, Offline) → Thaw 24 19:00. Away page (SESSION_UX §4): "One day.
Rain." Then at most seven lines. What are they?

FIRST_REGION §5's baseline names events on Thaw 24–26 (Fenn's Mill seized) — but this player paid
Bram's Quarter-day through V1, so `debt_called(Fenn's)` is cleared (§7's Thaw 20 away page confirms
it) and the seizure never fires. Thaw 24 therefore has no scripted event.

`[HOLE: what the five powers do on an unscripted day. LIVING_WORLD §3 specifies a planner that runs
"each dawn, each power, in a fixed order" and picks a move by scoring goals. FIRST_REGION §4 instead
gives each power a **dated list of opening moves** (Thaw 1, 3, 7, 12, 16, 20, 24, 25, 26, 30, 32, 40,
45, 60, 70, 75, 82). Five powers over ninety days is 450 power-days; §5's baseline table shows about
twenty-five moves. The documents never say which artefact is authoritative — if the planner runs,
the dated lists are outputs that must move when the hero diverges the state, and nothing says they
do; if the dated lists are the plan, the planner in LIVING_WORLD §3 is decoration. Either way there
is **no worked example of the engine or the simulation producing anything on an ordinary day**, and
that is where 425 of the 450 power-days and 30–48 of the season's 40–60 quests (ADVENTURE_ENGINE §8)
have to come from.]`

I press on by assuming the away page says something bland. Journal: nothing is open in the trace
between Thaw 23 and Thaw 27. So the errand has to be a system action. Thaw 24 is a Stillday
(FIRST_REGION §5, flags) and the hero owes a **Stand** day at Wickery's wall (§7 hour 5:30: "one day
in six at the wall (Stilldays), 15 bits a day").

`[HOLE: what a Stand day is as play. CLASSES_AND_STANDING §2 defines Stand only as a clash weight
("the Fighter's presence at a site counts as half a band"); FIRST_REGION §7 makes it a paid contract
with a rota and a docking penalty for missing it (hour 7:25: "Dell docks a day's pay"). No document
says what the player does during a Stand day, how long it takes in real minutes, whether it is
abstracted like a rest, or on which of the nine screens it happens. It is the Fighter's Hand-tier
income and there is no play in it.]`

I substitute an errand: map-jump Wickery → Sallowford (3 h, LIVING_WORLD §1), arriving Thaw 24 22:00,
to hand Idony something. Idony's schedule (FIRST_REGION §3 row 2) is 8–12, 12–14, 14–18, 18–20.

`[HOLE: night hours are unassigned for most named NPCs. LIVING_WORLD §2 says a schedule is "a table
of hour-ranges → (site, activity)" and the hourly tick "places the NPC per schedule." Garrow Tull
(row 1) closes his day with "21–6 home"; Idony (2), Reyne (8), Aud (14), Marrock (18), Merrin (21),
Dell (22) and Sabel (17) simply stop, leaving 8–12 unassigned hours a day with no stated default. The
NPCs must be somewhere for the meeting/memory tick to run and for a player who arrives at night.]`

**Three-minute session: fails to complete.** Three holes before the first decision, and the one
worked example in the bible is a mid-campaign session printed twice.

### 2.2 The fifteen-minute session

**H2, The Herb-wife's Guest (ADVENTURE_ENGINE §7), Thaw 13, played as a Mage** — the bible traces
only a Fighter, so this is where I test the other two-thirds of the class design.

08:00, Sallowford. Away page. Hild's schedule (FIRST_REGION §3 row 6) is 6–9 gathering at the Wend
edge, 9–18 her house. At 09:00 she is home. Her door: Ilune in the drying-loft, grey to the wrists;
Drusk Fennick and five Chain blades arrive at 15:00 (H2 *Inputs*: "the Chain arrives at 15:00 on Thaw
13 by the Stair band's march time" — good, that is a real derivation from LIVING_WORLD §3's *Send
band*). Four branches offered: talk, move her by night, hold the door, give her up.

**Branch: move her by night.** It is 09:00 and the Chain arrive at 15:00.

`[HOLE: there is no wait or pass-time action. LIVING_WORLD §1 gives exactly three time advances —
open play at two real minutes per world hour, map travel (instant to the player), and rest (to the
next dawn, 6:00). "By night" is therefore reachable only by standing in the field for twelve real
minutes or by a rest that overshoots to Thaw 14 06:00, after the Chain have been and gone. The same
hole blocks V1's Rogue branch ("cut the slide-ropes at night") and H2's own "she could not be
here."]`

**Branch: use Mist to move her by day.** CLASSES_AND_STANDING §2's verb table gives the Mage's
answer to *hide* as "Mist and move by night."

`[HOLE: the field verbs have no resolution rule of any kind. Mist is specified only as a combat
ability — "fog over the party for three seconds; 25 water; 20 s cooldown" (CLASSES §2). Used as a
field verb it has no duration, no cost, no target, no failure state. This is not local to Mist: **all
twelve field verbs across the three classes — Break, Carry, Drill, Stand, Dowse, Read, Dry/Flood,
Ease, Listen, Shadow, Forge, Fence — are given a fictional effect and no mechanic.** There is no
check, no attribute, no difficulty, no time cost and no failure condition anywhere in the bible. The
one place failure is acknowledged, "a failed Forge is a weight-8 memory with the factor" (CLASSES §2),
never says what makes a Forge fail. I searched every file: the only probabilistic outcome in the
whole design outside combat is N2's "20% chance by the Stair's temper," which is itself an
unexplained number.]`

**Branch: talk.** H2: "Drusk searches anyway unless the hero's Stair regard ≥ +15 or a Rogue *Forge*
produces a passage-writ." A Mage at this point in the trace is at Stair +5 (V2's delivery). So the
talk fails.

`[HOLE: H2 specifies the success condition for Talk and never the failure result. ADVENTURE_ENGINE §5
requires every outcome to write at least one site state, one memory and (at Hand or above) one regard,
and states "the engine refuses an outcome whose consequence list is empty." The failed-Talk branch
of a Hand-tier quest has no consequence list at all — the player is left standing in Hild's doorway
with Drusk on the path and no rule for what happens next.]`

**Branch: hold the door.** H2 calls it "Even at level 4 with a companion, because Osk's militia band
stands at the other doors and counts in the clash." Osk is *hurt* Thaw 2–12 (FIRST_REGION §3 row 5),
so on Thaw 13 he is up — consistent, good. But a Mage who skipped V1 has **no companion**: Corva only
joins at Fenn's Mill after V1 (FIRST_REGION §7 hour 3:00), and V1 is optional. Solo, the fight is
Drusk (threat 38) plus five Chainmen (16 each) = **ER 118** against a level-4 solo Mage,
`PR = 10 + 16 + 5 = 31` — Overmatched by 3.8× (COMBAT §1, Ratings).

`[HOLE: the engine offers quests by standing tier alone (ADVENTURE_ENGINE §4) with no check on party
size or party rating. COMBAT §6(e) promises "nothing in the world — a quest, a door, a standing
threshold, a reward — requires an Overmatched win," and H2 keeps that promise only if the remaining
branches are live: talk is regard-gated and fails, move-by-night has no time mechanic, and give-her-up
costs Quiet −40, Hild permanently at *hates* (her healing closes for the campaign) and Ilune
imprisoned. The solo Mage's only unblocked branch is the one with a permanent penalty.]`

I take the trace's path instead — move her up the Wend path with a companion, eight hours, arrive at
the Rudd Stones 17:00, Sarane's weight-9 memory, Quiet +15, Ilune joins. That works, reads well, and
ends with a nameable world change. **Fifteen-minute session: completes on one of four branches, with
four holes on the others.**

### 2.3 The evening session

**Thaw 20–23, the Sunk Court, played as a Rogue** — again, the bible traces a Fighter (FIRST_REGION
§7 evening four) and writes Encounter B for a Mage (COMBAT §7B). The Rogue is untested and the Court
is where I expected it to be.

**Thaw 20, 14:00, Wickery.** Kit Ashby on the quay with Corrow's letter: **N1**.

*Inconsistency:* Kit's schedule (FIRST_REGION §3 row 20) is 6–10 the quay, 10–16 the Shelf ("Corrow's
Reach on letter days"), 16–22 the Post's back room. At 14:00 on a letter day she is at Corrow's
Reach. Both N1's journal text ("Kit Ashby put a letter in my hand on the quay") and FIRST_REGION §7
hour 7:25 put her on the quay at Thaw 20 afternoon. The schedule system's first real test in the
trace contradicts the schedule.

Supplies: three flasks of oil (§7 hour 7:25). FIRST_REGION §6 "What runs out": two flasks for a
layer-1 run, **four for the Salt Hall**, and salt air halves a flask's four hours (COMBAT §3) to two.
The traced descent goes to the Salt Hall on three flasks and never runs dry. Minor, but this is the
resource the dungeon is built on.

**Thaw 21, dusk, the outer stones.** Corrow, two Wrack, a lamp. Escorted descent to layer 1, the
Drowned Steps (FIRST_REGION §6): salt-hounds in packs of two to four, salt-cranes, mud at half speed
off the flagstones, **and a husk at the inner stair.**

`[HOLE / rule violation: the Salted husk has no answer on the Rogue's arc. COMBAT §1: husk 120
health, armour 3, immune to Bind and Snare, telegraphs every hit; "Weak to: Wring ×2; Crack/Break
strips crust." Wring is Mage-only; Break is Fighter-only; Crack belongs to the Shield companion role
(Gulla, Osk). On Thaw 21 a Rogue's party is Corva (Bow: Pin) and Ilune (Draw: Fling, Bind, Ease —
**no Wring**); Gulla is not met until Thaw 27 and Osk "will walk with the hero only within the
valley" (FIRST_REGION §3 row 5), which the Shelf is not. **COMBAT §2 rule 2 states: "No enemy is
immune to a whole class. Immunities are to one ability, and the counter is always on the same class's
arc (a husk ignores Bind; Wring kills it)." Its own worked example is a Mage sentence, and the rule
is broken by the region's signature enemy against the Rogue.** The Rogue's only recourse is 9-damage
Cuts into armour 3.]`

`[HOLE: the order of armour and multipliers is unspecified. COMBAT §1: "Armour subtracts flat from
every hit"; the Rogue's "every Cut from behind is a *Mark* at ×2" and Slip's follow-up is ×3. Against
the husk (armour 3) a tier-2 Mark is either (9×2)−3 = 15 or (9−3)×2 = 12; against a salt-warden
(armour 5) it is 13 or 8, and against a stone-warden (armour 6) 12 or 6 — a 50–100% swing on every
boss in the region, decided by an unwritten line.]`

I assume the generous order and grind the husk down. Layer 2, the Salt Hall stair: Encounter B
(COMBAT §7B) is written for a Mage and replayed by FIRST_REGION §7 hour 9:20 for a Fighter using
Break to strip the crust. Neither instrument exists here. The Rogue's route through the Salt Hall is
not specified in any file.

**Layer 3, the Well.** The party has the well-ring intact, so the Well is sealed. But
ADVENTURE_ENGINE Vo2 offers a region-one branch that opens it: "Take the bronze and leave the well:
`layer3_open`, the Well becomes the region's deepest dungeon."

`[HOLE: layer 3 is not designed. FIRST_REGION §6's table gives it one row — "the nine (Salted, each a
salt-warden's strength; they wake one at a time)", resources "none", return "none: the sluice above,
or a flooded chamber, or a Stonereader who can Read what the nine want." No rooms, no map, no
encounter entries (COMBAT §9 counts "11 dungeon rooms" for the region; FIRST_REGION §8 counts "4
layers, 23 rooms" — the two do not reconcile), and its one stated non-combat exit requires a Mage
subclass taken at level 15, which the fairness model (PROGRESSION §5) puts at hour 50, two regions
later. FIRST_REGION §8 names the risk honestly — "the region ships with it sealed and one way to open
it" — while Vo2 ships the branch that opens it. Nine salt-wardens at threat 110 is ER 990 against a
level-8 party's PR ~156.]`

**Thaw 23, return to Wickery.** The aftermath scene is excellent and needs no invention: Marrock
refuses the Court's bronze, Kit buys it in the back room, Drusk is asking where the hero slept, and
Merrin Hale's notice on the board has the hero's name in it. A nameable world change, and the best
five minutes in the bible.

**Evening session: completes, with the dungeon's signature enemy unfightable by one of three classes
and its deepest layer undesigned.**

**Score of the table: 12/12 name people with reasons; 11/12 place the stake on the map; 9/12 write a
complete consequence set on every branch; 12/12 differ in shape.** The shape differentiation is
genuinely excellent — a search, a courier run, a two-cause investigation, a list of names, a hidden
person against a clock, a witnessing contest, an escorted descent by an enemy, a job offer, a seizure
at a door, two letters and a clash, a flooding choice, a town assault. No two are the same quest.

Three weaknesses across the set:

1. **`choose` appears in six of twelve.** It is the engine's most-used verb and it is not a verb — it
   is the fact that a quest has branches. `break` appears in none of the twelve, though V1's Fighter
   solution uses it.
2. **Twist families repeat inside the showcase.** T23 twice verbatim, T01 twice; the companion-agenda
   family carries three of twelve (H2, N2, Vo2). §6's rule only forbids a family "twice in a row," so
   this is legal — and it means the rule is too weak to prevent what it is for.
3. **All twelve sit on dated anchor events** — the dam, the Quarter-day, the levy, the Parley, the
   Address, the Build, the Raid. ADVENTURE_ENGINE §8 claims 40–60 quests a season. None of the other
   28–48 is shown, and none of the twelve demonstrates the trigger-to-quest path from an unremarkable
   state, which is where the engine actually has to work.

### 3.2 Reverse-engineering: quests thirteen and fourteen

I tried to generate two more using **only** the documented grammar and tables. Here is what happened.

**The tables do not exist.** ADVENTURE_ENGINE §1 sizes them: "Vael 140 given + 80 bynames; Skerrow 60
+ 3 holds; Ulder 40 + rings; Kest 30 family + 60 given; 48 motives; 30 twists; 22 site types; 14
opposition kinds; 60 rumour templates." §8 sizes them again: "48 motives × 4 peoples × 3 phrasings =
576 lines; 30 twists × 2 lines; 22 site types × 6 description lines; 60 rumour templates; 60
map-event templates; 14 notice templates: about 1,200 authored lines." **None of them is printed.**

## 4. The standing trace

Followed through CLASSES_AND_STANDING §5–6 and FIRST_REGION §7. At each milestone: what did the
player *do*, and how does the *world* show it?

**Milestone 1 — Villager, renown 0.** Earned by nothing; the hero has a trade and no destiny
(CLASSES §1: "Standing at creation is 0"). World shows: neighbours give errands. **Honest, and
correctly not a milestone at all.**

**Milestone 2 — Hand, renown 20, hour ~4.** *Did:* V3 (Tobbin's flock, Thaw 1), V1 (the dam and the
river, Thaw 7–8), V2 (Sallowford's coin to Wickery and Pell's letter home, Thaw 9–11). *World shows:*
Reyne — who has never met the hero — uses the *heard* line on Thaw 2 because Tobbin told him at the
Gorse End yard (LIVING_WORLD §2's worked example, seen from the player's side); Jory Quill names the
hero to their face at Thaw 14; the hearth glyph goes ember → flame in the player's hand on Thaw 11.
**Earned by deeds, measured by heads, shown by mouths. This is exactly what the brief asked for and
it is the best thing in the bible.**

*But the arithmetic is wrong at the first step.* CLASSES §5's worked example gives V3 as "Tobbin (1),
Reyne (5, via kin write), Hild (5), Garrow (2), two folk (1 + 1)" = **15**. FIRST_REGION §7 hour 0:10
gives the same quest as "Standing 12 (Tobbin 1, Reyne 5, Hild 5, a folk 1)." Two different lists of
who remembers, for the same event, in two files.

*And the tier rule contradicts itself.* FIRST_REGION §7 hour 3:00: "Standing 27 — the hearth glyph is
an ember still (Hand needs 20 with the tier read at dawn)." CLASSES §5, Tick: "Recomputed at dawn
**and after every deed**." STATUS.json logs this as an open question about wording; it is not — it is
a rule and a trace saying opposite things about when the player's tier changes.

**Milestone 3 — Name, renown 100, hour ~7.** *Did:* H2 (hid and moved Ilune), H1 (took the levy
himself), plus the Wickery quay's rumour. *World shows:* Marrock walks up the valley to find the hero
— "a thing that does not happen"; Idren offers work; Corrow writes; the Post inn's greeting names the
hero; prices move. **Shown superbly.** *Measured badly:* the trace goes 74 → **146** between Thaw 11
and Thaw 14 and never itemises the +72. The named heads gained in those three days are Ilune (2),
Sarane (20), Dell Coombe (5), Marrock (2) and a few levy folk — call it 35. CLASSES §5's own example
says the quay-and-inn rumour is worth "+30 in a week" and dates it to **Thaw 16**, two days after the
trace has already banked it.

**Milestone 4 — Voice, renown 500, hour ~17.** *Did:* led the Moot's men and held the Rudd Bridge
(Vo1) — an unambiguous, earned, world-scale deed, played as a clash rather than rolled. *World shows:*
letters from Brenning and Fallgate; Marrock offers a Skerrow contract; Corrow writes a second time;
Merrin's notice at every board; the glyph becomes a beacon. **Nothing here is granted by fiat, by
level or by a quest merely completing.**

*But the measurement collapses.* FIRST_REGION §7 hour 15:00: "by the 36th every tradesperson in
Wickery has passed it (weight 3, ~60 × 2 reach) and half the folk. **Standing recomputed at dawn on
the 36th: 579. Voice.**" That is roughly +305 renown in two days from one notice board — 60
tradespeople at reach 2 and ~185 folk at reach 1.

Two documents forbid it. LIVING_WORLD §2: "Unnamed folk ... are drawn from name tables when the
engine needs them to speak, and then become named" — so unpromoted folk are not entities and cannot
hold a memory. LIVING_WORLD §5 budgets the simulation at "8 slots × (25 named + up to 60 promoted) =
680 memory slots maximum." **Sixty promoted folk, not two hundred and forty-five.**

Worse, I added up the ceiling. If *every* named NPC in FIRST_REGION §3 held a memory of the hero —
all twenty-five, including both reach-100 leaders, Corrow and Brakka — that is **368 renown**. Add all
sixty permitted promoted folk at reach 1–2: **428 to 488.** Add High Reeve Tull (100): **528 to 588.**

So: **Voice (floor 500) requires nearly every simulated head in the region, and Force (floor 2,000) is
about four times the entire region's ceiling.** Five regions of the same size would total ~2,150,
meaning Force requires that the hero personally be remembered by essentially every person in the
Hearth, simultaneously, while CLASSES §5 also specifies that "renown *falls* when memories decay:
weight ≤ 5 memories decay in ten-day steps." Over a 200-hour campaign the decay alone makes 2,000
unreachable.

**Milestone 5 — Force, renown 2,000.** Not traced. CLASSES §6's row says "Brenning's and Fallgate's
memories; a site held (sway ≥ 60); a pass or a court changed," and PROGRESSION §5 asserts "Force at
about 150 hours falls out of the curve." The brief asked for the road traced at five milestones; four
are traced and the fifth is a table row with a number no population can produce.

**One more fiat.** FIRST_REGION §7 hour 10:30 crosses the Moot's Address threshold with "regard +20 —
H1's 15 and a Stand contract's 5." A Stand contract giving +5 Moot regard appears in no rule. It
would live in the deed table — LIVING_WORLD §4: "The table has 64 entries in the first region ... each
entry gives the act tag, the base memory weight, which NPCs and powers receive the memory, the regard
deltas per power, the standing change (via reach) and the site state it writes." **That table is
counted and never printed.** Every regard number, every memory weight and every renown figure in the
twelve quests and the twenty-hour trace is therefore an authorial assertion, not a derivation.

**Standing trace verdict: the road is earned and the world shows it at every milestone — the design
intent is exactly right and beautifully expressed in fiction. The measurement that is supposed to make
it non-arbitrary is arbitrary, because its two source tables (deeds, reach-by-head) are unprinted and
its two upper tiers exceed the population the simulation is budgeted to hold.**

## 5. The eight rubric lines

### Line 1 — World: **8.0**

Original, coherent and genuinely comprehensible; four peoples, five powers, three ages exactly
(WORLD §2.2–2.4); the map has one shape and §2.1 teaches you to draw it in one sentence. History is
load-bearing rather than decorative — §2.2's "Why the history still matters in play" plus the
age-tagging rule in ADVENTURE_ENGINE §3 put the three ages inside ordinary quests. Every Part Two
heading quotes the one-page line it grows from, and the contract holds. The friend test passes: I
explained it in 110 seconds without notes. Against it: **the Still's continued fall has no cause
anywhere in the bible**, and it is the clock that surfaces every dungeon and starts every dispute;
Part One uses "the Stair" for both the road and the company with no disambiguating clause; Part One's
"the only way anyone gets in" is contradicted by §2.2's Skerrow mining in from outside; the page
carries no hint that anything in this world will attack you; and the stated length is wrong — the
page is **535 words**, not the 474 claimed in §"Part One"'s footer.
*To 8.5:* state why the lake still falls; disambiguate the Stair on the page; soften the
"only way in" absolute; add one clause about what walks on the Shelf; fix the word count.

### Line 2 — Living world: **7.5**

All four minimums are present with inputs, state, tick, outputs and a worked example each — the file
honours its own format without a single exception, which is rarer than it should be. The baseline
season in FIRST_REGION §5 — ninety days of the valley with nobody playing, clash odds stated and rolls
shown (Thaw 30–35: "odds 10/50 — rolled 61, Moot loses"; Thaw 82: "odds 60/150 — rolled 22, Wrack
wins") — is the single strongest artefact in the bible and is exactly the proof the brief demands
that factions act without the hero. Memory decay bands, reach, greeting tiers, price and access,
door flags and the never-pruned deed ledger are all specified. Against it: the **planner-vs-dated-list
seam** (LIVING_WORLD §3 runs a goal-scoring planner every dawn; FIRST_REGION §4 gives fixed-date
opening moves; 450 power-days in a season and ~25 shown, with no rule for the rest and no statement
of which artefact is authoritative); night hours unassigned for seven of twenty-five NPCs
(FIRST_REGION §3); `reach` defined as "how many people this NPC talks to in a week" but never used as
a propagation rate — the tick uses a flat 0.3 on schedule collisions (LIVING_WORLD §2), so the fiction
and the mechanic never meet; the 680-slot memory budget in §5 cannot hold the population the Voice
crossing consumes; and **the 64-entry deed table, the 60 map-event templates and the 60 rumour
templates are counted and never written**, so every number they would produce is asserted.
*To 8.5:* print the deed table; pick one of the planner and the dated list and derive the other from
it, with one worked ordinary day; close the schedule hours; reconcile the memory budget with the
renown the trace claims.

### Line 3 — Adventure engine: **7.0**

The architecture is right and I would build it: trigger-first from a site state or NPC condition
rather than slot-filling left to right (§2 — the single decision that stops this being a "kill ten
wolves" generator); place is always the cause, never a backdrop (§3); age tagging that forces the
history into ordinary quests; shape signature for repetition (§6); "If nothing fits, the engine offers
nothing — a quiet day is better than a stale one," which is the only honest failure mode a generator
has; anchors expressed as site states with door flags rather than scripts (§6). The twelve quests are
excellent reading, name people with reasons, sit on the map, and come out in twelve genuinely
different shapes. Against it: **none of the tables the engine draws from exists** — 13 of 48 motives
named, 11 of 30 twists, 0 of 22 site types, 0 of 14 opposition kinds, 0 of 60 rumour templates, 0 of
the four text tables §3 assembles prose from — and my attempt at quests thirteen and fourteen (§3.2)
could only succeed by writing them myself, which is the engine failing its own test in my hands.
`choose` carries six of twelve quests; `break` carries none; T23 and T01 each appear twice; all twelve
sit on dated anchors, so the ~30–48 quests a season that come from ordinary state are never
demonstrated; and three quests have branches whose consequence lists violate §5's own no-empty-outcome
rule (V2's "go round by Gorse End", V3's give-up, H2's failed Talk).
*To 8.5:* print all 48 motives with three-per-people phrasings for at least twelve of them, all 30
twists, the 22 site types and the 14 opposition kinds; then generate six quests from undated ordinary
state and show the inputs, including at least one using *break* as its verb.

### Line 4 — Classes and standing: **6.5**

The three classes are genuinely distinct in combat (Stamina/Water/chained-Stamina; Hold vs Bind vs
Slip; four arcs each with one job apiece per COMBAT §2 rule 4), and the explicit **cannot** lists are
first-rate design — "A Fighter alone in the Court's flooded halls is a Fighter who needs a companion"
is a design rule doing real work. The subclass roadmap is concrete and each class states how the base
leaves the room empty (CLASSES §2: "The base keeps Slip visible, Forge one-shot and travel at map
speed to leave these rooms empty"). Standing-as-renown — Σ reach over heads that hold a memory,
unspendable, ungrindable, falling only by being forgotten — is the best single idea in the bible and
the road from villager to force is earned and visibly shown at four of five milestones. Against it:
**the twelve field verbs, which are the entire out-of-combat identity of all three classes, have no
resolution rule of any kind** — no check, no cost, no time, no failure condition, and the one
acknowledged failure ("a failed Forge is a weight-8 memory") never says what makes it fail; **eight of
those twelve never appear in any session example in the bible** (the smell hit, §6 below); the renown
arithmetic is asserted at Name (74 → 146 unexplained) and Voice (+305 in two days from folk the
simulation does not instantiate); the region's entire population ceiling is ~430–590 renown against a
Voice floor of 500 and a Force floor of 2,000; CLASSES §5's "recomputed at dawn and after every deed"
contradicts FIRST_REGION §7's renown-27 Villager; CLASSES §5 and FIRST_REGION §7 give different
renown for the same quest (15 vs 12); and milestone 5 is a table row with no trace.
*To 8.5:* give every field verb a resolution rule, a cost and a failure state; re-derive Name, Voice
and Force floors against a stated population and a stated decay rate; trace one milestone as a Mage or
a Rogue; reconcile the two V3 renown lists and the dawn-recompute rule.

### Line 5 — Combat, dungeons and autobattle: **7.5**

The most reliable file in the bible. Numbers with models throughout, and I checked them: every party
rating in COMBAT §7 and FIRST_REGION §7 recomputes correctly from `PR = Σ(10 + 4×level + 5×tier)`
(54, 127, 156, 110, 127 all verified), every encounter rating recomputes from the threat column (46,
86, 157, 72, 102 all verified), and the whole XP curve `60 × L^1.6` reproduces the trace's level-ups
to the point (level 8 needs 4,338; the trace ends at 4,343). Fifteen enemies with a role, a telegraph,
a class weakness and a threat number. The autobattle policy is written as an implementable priority
list; parity is defined operationally rather than rhetorically (Fair = what the policy wins above 40%
party health, 147 simulations a balance pass) and the six encounter runs are credible and consistent —
the three trades (2 s slower / 2 hp better; 2 s faster / 11 water poorer; 8 s slower / one more ledger
burned) are precisely the shape of an honest parity claim. Dungeon rules have reason, layers-by-state
rather than by number, four resources that run out, a return per layer and a real failure cost
(corpse-run, permanent scar, dead companions who stay dead). Against it: **the Salted husk breaks
COMBAT §2 rule 2 for the Rogue** — immune to Snare, and its two crust-strippers are the Fighter's
Break and the Shield's Crack — and the Salted are the region's signature enemy; the armour/multiplier
order is unstated and swings a Rogue's Mark 50–100% against every armoured boss; layer 3 of the Court
is one table row while ADVENTURE_ENGINE Vo2 ships the branch that opens it, and COMBAT §9's "11
dungeon rooms" does not reconcile with FIRST_REGION §8's "4 layers, 23 rooms"; and the two documented
autobattle divergences both leave the world *worse* (Encounter C burns a second ledger; FIRST_REGION
§7's Thaw 34 kills Drusk where a hand spares him, forfeiting a weight-9 memory and closing N2's
`chain_sergeant_absent` trigger). That last is inside the brief's "expression and edge cases", but it
is one instance from being outside it, because expression has become world state.
*To 8.5:* give the Rogue a crust answer on its own arc; state the armour/multiplier order; design
layer 3 or close Vo2's branch; and add one autobattle divergence that leaves the world *better*, so
the trade is symmetrical.

### Line 6 — Economy, session and UX: **7.5**

The store discipline is the best I have read in a document like this. The test — "every store line
must name the in-world way to get the same thing, or be cosmetic" (PROGRESSION §6) — is a rule a
designer can apply to a new line in ten seconds, and the not-sold list is exhaustive and correct
("experience, coin, marks, materials, gear, companions, standing, regard, knacks, respecs, revives,
scar removal, Salt easing, time skips, offline-cap raises, quest re-rolls, map reveals, difficulty
changes, autobattle ... and anything with a timer, key, pull, chest or pity attached"). Anti-grind is
structural rather than exhortative (§7: renown counts heads not deeds, so "a hundred errands for
Tobbin are one memory in one head"). The fairness model has a real hour-by-hour table with a stated
pace model behind it. The portrait screen is specified in millimetres with a mirroring rule, a stated
one-thumb cost that the design accepts rather than hides, and a genuine accessibility position
(autobattle as the accessibility mode, not a concession). Against it: **the bible contains exactly one
three-minute session and prints it twice** (SESSION_UX §3 and FIRST_REGION §7's Thaw 11 check-in), and
it is a mid-campaign session — the first hour, which is the three minutes on which the game will
actually be judged, is a 45-minute block by the trace's own clock; **there is no wait or pass-time
action**, so every "at night" solution in the twelve quests is unreachable; the creation screen is one
of nine named screens and has no specification; the Stand contract is the Fighter's Hand-tier income
with no play, no screen and no decision in it; and the two ledgers disagree (PROGRESSION §1's 4,370
vs FIRST_REGION §7's 4,343 XP; WORLD §2.1's eight miles Sallowford–Wickery vs FIRST_REGION §1's
segments summing to eleven).
*To 8.5:* specify creation and a wait action; write a real hour-one three-minute session and a second
three-minute session that is not the Thaw 11 one; give Stand a decision; reconcile the two ledgers.

### Line 7 — Feasibility and honesty: **7.5**

Volumes are counted everywhere and the counting is specific: 40 sites × 6 description lines, 25 NPCs
× ~375 schedule rows, 15 enemies × 3 silhouettes, 38 field encounters + 11 dungeon rooms + 3 set
pieces, 64 deed entries, ~1,200 engine lines, 40 gear items, 9 screens (FIRST_REGION §8, and a "Sizes
and risks" section closing every system file). Team and time are stated with a month-by-month plan and
a real acceptance test — "the baseline in §5 must reproduce" — which is the right acceptance test.
Risks are named with mitigations in every file, including the ones a designer is tempted to hide (the
engine reading as a wheel by Green; the one-thumb tap; the business risk: "a game that sells colours
and valleys earns less per player than one that sells power; the design accepts it"). This is
unusually honest work. Against it: **the counted tables are only counted.** The ~1,200 engine lines,
the 64 deeds, the 60 map events and the 60 rumours are the content that makes every other claim in
the bible true, and not one is started — so the counts are hopes, not estimates, and no comparable is
cited for any of them. Fourteen people and ten months for a faction simulation, a quest engine, a
real-time action layer, 40 sites, a four-layer dungeon and nine screens on device, with four artists,
is optimistic and undefended. Three contradictions are logged in STATUS.json as "left open" rather than
fixed, and one of them (the standing recompute) is a rule contradicting a trace, not an open question.
FIRST_REGION §8 promises layer 3 ships sealed while Vo2 ships the branch that opens it. And **six of
the seven offline advances in the twenty-hour trace do not obey LIVING_WORLD §1's own four-world-hours-
per-real-hour rule**: Thaw 9 12:00 + 19 real hours is Thaw 12 16:00, not the stated Thaw 11 07:00 (33
hours out); the others miss by 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 hours. The trace was written backwards from the dated
events it needed to hit, which is the same seam as line 2's planner problem.
*To 8.5:* write ten percent of every counted table so the counts become estimates; re-run the trace's
clock from the rule; cite a comparable for the fourteen-person schedule; close the four contradictions
rather than logging them.

### Line 8 — Design-smell checklist: **5.0** — one hit

is an automatic fail regardless of the other seven lines.

## 8. Verdict

**Fail.** The lowest line is **classes and standing at 6.5**, dragged down not by its design — the
renown system is the best idea in this bible and the road from villager to force is genuinely earned
and genuinely shown — but because its two measuring instruments, the deed table and the reach-by-head
count, are one unprinted and one arithmetically incapable of reaching its own upper tiers. **Line 8
also fails outright on one smell hit:** the out-of-combat identity of the Mage and the Rogue is
carried entirely by eight field verbs that appear in no session example and have no resolution rule.
The whole set sits closer to a studio-buildable design than a first round usually does: the world
passes the friend test in under two minutes, the baseline season proves factions act without the hero
in a way I have rarely seen written down, the combat file's arithmetic reproduces everywhere I checked
it, the autobattle parity claim is defined operationally rather than argued, and the store discipline
is publishable as it stands. What separates it from buildable is one repeated habit rather than nine
different failures: **the bible counts its tables instead of writing them, and traces its clock
instead of running it.** Print the motives, the twists and the deeds; give the field verbs rules; and
re-run the twenty-hour trace from the clock rule rather than backwards from the dates it wanted to
hit — and lines 2, 3, 4 and 7 all move together. That is one round of work, not three.
