## 1. The map in words

Stand on the Ring Road at Wickery and look west, up the valley. Behind you, four miles of Shelf to the water. Ahead, the Rudd comes down out of the Wend in a straight green line, and the spoke road follows its south bank. The valley is thirty miles long and never more than six wide, and every place in it is on the river or the road.

**From the top down:**

- **Rudd's Head** — the pass. Snow until Mid Thaw. The road over it goes nowhere the game reaches this region.
- **Thrum** — the Skerrow hold cut into the cliff below the pass: a gate, a hall, galleries running back into the Rim, a forge that never goes out. Hold-mother Brakka; four hundred Skerrow; iron. Twenty-five miles and two days' walk from Wickery, and the region's only site the player reaches on foot rather than by map jump, because the last six miles are Skerrow stair.
- **The Rudd Stones** — nine stones on the Shoulder above the Wend, over the Rudd's upper water, an hour's climb by a marked path from the Wend track and half a day below Thrum. Kept by Sarane and Ondrin. A stone-warden sleeps at its centre.
- **The Rudd Wend** — eight miles of forest where the road narrows to a track along the river. Ulder ground off the track. Four miles above Sallowford, on the north bank, **the Hewers' Camp**: Thrum's timber crew, a slide down to the river, and in this Thaw a dam. On the south bank, an hour above Gorse End's pasture, the strip of Wend that ends at **the old ash**, a stone-mark.
- **Fenn's Mill** — where the Wend ends: Bram Fenwright's mill on a race off the Rudd, two miles above the village. The valley's flour.
- **Sallowford** — the home village. Thirty-one souls. A ford (and Osk's ferry-boat for when the Rudd runs high), the Ford Inn, Garrow Tull's smithy, Hild Marrow's house with the drying-loft, Idony Sallow's reeve's table, twelve houses, a green, a well the Ulder bless at Firstwater, a notice post. Above it on the south slope, **Gorse End**: Reyne Gorse's big farm and the pasture where Tobbin keeps the flock.
- **Hobb's Cross** — three miles down, where the spoke road meets a farm lane: Wat Hobb's inn and cart-yard, a barn, a stable that hires a mule. The crossroads is where the Wrack watch the road.
- **The Rudd Bridge** — three miles on: the stone bridge where the spoke road meets the Ring Road, and the Rudd, now wide and slow, crosses under. Every cart from the valley crosses it. The Stair wants a toll-house on its Wickery end. The **Old Shore road** begins at its southern abutment.
- **Wickery** — the town, four hundred souls, two miles further, on the Present Shore where the Rudd meets the Shelf: a wall (earth and timber, defence 30), the reeve's house, the Moot's watch-house, the Stair's counting-house with its yard and the Chain's barracks, Marrock of Thrum's iron-yard, the Post inn on the quay, the notice board at the market cross, the quay itself with the **low-mark post** (a notched timber that shows the Still's fall, year by year), and the boats drawn up on the mud below.
- **The western Shelf** — beyond the quay, four miles of cracked mud and reed to the water. On it: **the Salt Pans** (the Stair's boiling-sheds, where Shelf water is evaporated for salt), the **Old Shore road** (the drowned-age track along the first-age shoreline, which is the Shelf's inner edge; it leaves the Ring Road at the Rudd Bridge and runs four miles west across the mud, and sheep are grazed on its verges in Thaw before the valley grass comes), **Corrow's Reach** (the Wrack's camp: turf huts round a fire in a hollow, a palisade of driftwood, defence 10), and **the Sunk Court** (§6), a ring of stone halls half out of the silt, surfaced in 605 C. From the Old Shore road's second mile the Court's roofs and the Wrack's smoke are visible across flat mud, which is how a villager who has never left the valley first learns the Court is there.

The eleven miles the round-one draft summed to were three segments of four, four and two; the segments are three, three and two, and the total is the eight miles [WORLD](../world/WORLD.md) §2.8 and [LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §1 have always said.

**When travel is a map jump and when it is walked.** Map travel is instant for the player and costs only world hours ([LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §1). It is offered **only between sites the hero has already stood in, and only when the hero is travelling alone or with the party**. A first traversal is walked in the open at two real minutes to the world hour, and so is any move where the hero is **escorting, droving, carrying or leading** — a flock, a hunted Ulder, a levy, a flour cart, a downed companion. That rule is why the trace in §7 spends its real minutes where it does, and it is the reason the region's roads are content rather than a loading screen.

Sites in the region: 14 named above plus 26 minor (farmsteads, fords, Wend clearings, Shelf pans and reed-beds): 40 in the engine's tables.

## 2. Why this valley is the first region

It is a cross-section of the one page: Rim, Shoulders, Wend, Lowmark, Shelf and water in a day's walk; all four peoples living where they live (Skerrow at Thrum, Ulder at the stones and in the Wend, Vael in the village and town, Kest in the counting-house); four powers present and the fifth (the Stair's seat) reachable by letter; the Age of Stone in the Court, the Cutting in the Skerrow contract, the Age of Ships in the ledger. And it is the valley the hero was born in, so the first Hand deeds are for neighbours whose names the player already knows.

### The two heads worth a hundred, and what a leader's word is worth

This region has two NPCs at reach 100 — Halse Corrow and Brakka of Thrum — and one more, High Reeve Tull at Brenning, reachable from the valley by letter. Between them they are 300 of the region's 528–588 renown ceiling ([CLASSES_AND_STANDING](../systems/CLASSES_AND_STANDING.md) §5), so the question of what it costs to reach one is the question of whether the whole standing system is honest. The round-one draft let Corrow write from a meeting, which put a quarter of the crossing to Voice inside one conversation. Three answers, and the region settles them here.

**One: the number has a model.** Corrow is not worth a hundred heads because he leads a faction. He is worth a hundred because he was **Reeve of Wickery for eleven years until 606**, and the reach he kept is countable: about sixty Wrack on the Shelf who take his word as an order, and about forty in Wickery and on the Ring Road who were his people first and still answer him — Kit Ashby's boats, four of Wat Hobb's carters, the quaymen, and every debtor family whose son is out on the mud. A hundred heads a week, and the model is his biography. Brakka's hundred is the same arithmetic in a different shape: four hundred Skerrow at Thrum, of whom about a hundred are in the hall or the galleries in a week where the Hold-mother speaks, and a Skerrow repeats a name that was spoken before a witness. Nobody else in the valley is above 20, and no role is worth 100 by being a role: a leader who has no ear is a notable.

**Two: the leader rule already forbids the cheap road.** A reach-100 NPC takes a hero-memory only from a deed at their own seat, a deed that moves one of their power's three ranked goals, or a letter they wrote themselves ([LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §2). No rumour reaches them; no notice reaches them; no meeting tick passes them anything. A hundred errands in Sallowford move Corrow by nothing at all.

**Three — the region's own clause, and the answer to "one meeting is too cheap": meeting Corrow writes nothing.** Standing at the outer stones with him at dusk on the 21st is **row 67 `came_and_looked`**, weight 2, and weight 2 does not travel ([CLASSES_AND_STANDING](../systems/CLASSES_AND_STANDING.md) §5). What writes is what happens four hours later at the Salt Hall door, at 22:00 the same night: **row 26 `kept_the_charge`, weight 10, never decaying — the well-ring kept whole against a crew the Stair is paying**, which is the Wrack's third ranked goal and is done at the Court, which is Corrow's second seat, with Corrow himself standing there, so the leader rule's gate opens twice over ([LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §4). A hero who takes N1's letter, crosses four miles of Shelf, meets Corrow, hears him out and goes home has 277 minus 100: Corrow holds **row 67 `came_and_looked`**, weight 2, and the number under the glyph does not move. The hundred is the price of the dungeon, not of the conversation, and the ledger in §7.3a shows it landing at **22:00 on Thaw 21**, four hours after the meeting at the outer stones and not at it.

So the reach is right, and it is right for a stated reason rather than because leaders should feel important. **What a leader's word is worth is a hundred heads a week, once, for a thing done in front of them that they wanted done** — and there are three such people the hero can reach from this valley, which is why Voice is 350 and Force is not reachable here at all.

## 4. The five powers in the valley — three with a seat, two from its ends

Five powers act on the valley. Three have a face and a seat in it (the Moot through Aud Penhallow, the Stair through Vesk Idren, the Wrack through Halse Corrow); two act from its ends (the Holds from Thrum through Marrock, the Quiet from the Rudd Stones through Sarane and Ondrin). The round-one heading said "three powers present" and its body said five; the count was never in doubt and the heading was wrong.

**What the dated lists below are.** [LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §3's dawn planner is the authority: each power scores its three ranked goals, prices every legal move, takes the best and breaks ties on cost, in the fixed order Quiet, Holds, Moot, Stair, Wrack. The dates below are **the planner's output for the Thaw-1 state of this valley, run forward with nobody playing** — a projection printed so a reader can check the planner against it, not a script the planner obeys. Every date moves when the state moves, and §7 shows five of them moving: the dam is broken on Thaw 7 so the Holds' Thaw 26 *Fell* runs short; Fenn's debt is paid on Thaw 8 so the Thaw 24 *Seize* never fires and the Wrack's queued recruit never arrives; the Court's ring is held on Thaw 22 so the Thaw 28 cut and the Thaw 34 opening never happen; the toll-house is stopped on Thaw 34 so the Thaw 60 masons go home; and the Wrack's season-end move changes from *Raid* to *Parley* because a door the hero opened is set. **Each power's three goals are printed with it, because the deed table's two regard multipliers — ×2 at a goal site, ×3 for a goal ended — cannot be read without them.**

**The Moot, through Aud Penhallow at Wickery.** Resources in the valley: one militia band (quality 1) at Wickery's wall; Dell's watch (quality 1; 2 after a Drill); Sallowford's band (quality 1; Osk's); sway 70 at Wickery, 75 at Sallowford; no coin (the Moot's chest at Wickery holds 30 marks, all of it owed). **Goals, ranked:** (1) no toll-house at the Rudd Bridge; (2) the Wrack off the Ring Road; (3) the levy filled. **Opening moves:** Thaw 3 *Levy* at Wickery (six from each village, thirty days); Thaw 7 *Post* ("The Moot will pay for word of the Wrack's camps"); Thaw 27 *Address* to the hero at Name if regard ≥ +20; Thaw 32 *Send band* to the Rudd Bridge if the Stair builds (a clash the Moot loses unless the hero is in it).

**The Stair, through Vesk Idren at the counting-house.** Resources: coin 600 in the Wickery chest; the Chain (one band, quality 2, Drusk); the debt ledger (Fenn's Mill 3 marks Quarter-day and 8 arrears; Sallowford 3; eleven farms; Wickery's Moot chest 30); the Salt Pans; a contract with Thrum for salvage labour. **Goals, ranked:** (1) every valley debt called or paid by Reckoning; (2) salvage rights to the Sunk Court; (3) a toll-house at the Rudd Bridge. **Opening moves:** Thaw 1 Quarter-day collected (Sabel rides the valley); Thaw 3 *Call debt* on Fenn's Mill (late); Thaw 12 *Send band* to the Salt Pans (after Ilune's working); Thaw 20 *Salvage* at the Court (a Skerrow crew, paid); Thaw 24 *Seize* Fenn's Mill if unpaid; Thaw 30 *Build* the toll-house (five days, 60 marks, two Chain bands — the second raised Thaw 25); Thaw 70 *Call debt* on three farms after the Quarter-day interest doubles.

*One dawn, worked, so the list above can be checked against the planner rather than believed.* **Thaw 3, the Stair's turn, fourth in the fixed order.** Goal 1 is at distance 4 (four ledgers overdue) × rank weight 3 = **12**; goal 2 at distance 1 × 2 = **2**; goal 3 at distance 1 × 1 = **1**. Best is *Call debt* on Fenn's Mill, which is the Thaw 3 entry above, and it is the reason the miller's wheel and the miller's debt arrive in the same week — the planner did not arrange that, the dam did.

## 6. The dungeon — the Sunk Court

**Reason.** A court of the Age of Stone on the western Shelf: nine halls in a ring round a well, built when the bowl's floor was dry, drowned in the Drowning, and surfacing since 605 C. as the Still falls. The Wrack use its outer court as a second camp. The Stair pays Skerrow to cut its stone-bronze. The Holds want the bronze for themselves. The Quiet want it left, because the well-ring — the bronze band round the central well — is what holds the third layer sealed, and what is in the third layer is what the Court's Drawers became. Kit Ashby's brother drowned in the first layer in 607, when it was still wet.

**Layers.** Each is a state four powers have goals about.

Twenty-three rooms across four layers. The two counts have always described different objects and now say so.

### Layer 3, designed

Round one shipped layer 3 as one table row while [ADVENTURE_ENGINE](../systems/ADVENTURE_ENGINE.md) Vo2 shipped the branch that opens it. There are two ways it opens in region one and both are the player's own doing — N1's walk-away (the crew cuts the ring in four days) and Vo2's take-the-bronze-and-leave — so it is specified here to the standard of layers 1 and 2. Nothing in it needs a subclass.

**The five rooms.**
1. **The Ring Stair** — a spiral cut inside the well's own wall, four turns down, the cut stumps of the bronze band at every turn where the crew's chisels went in. One abreast. The Sump's water is audible from the second turn.
2. **The Names** — an antechamber whose western wall carries nine names in Age-of-Stone script and, under them, one line of preamble. A *Read* site (15 water, 30 minutes, 5 Salt — the stone is dry). The only room in the layer with nothing in it that wants the party dead, and the only place to sit.
3. **The Drum** — the round chamber, forty feet across, nine seated figures on a low bench facing the shaft. Its door admits two abreast; the Ring Stair admits one. That is the design's ceiling on how many of the nine a party ever fights at once: **never more than three, and only if three have woken.**
4. **The Sump** — the shaft's lip and, twenty feet down a wet ledge, the black water still in it. Drawing from the Sump is drawing from what the nine are holding: each 25 water taken wakes the next of them a day early, and the confirm sheet says so.
5. **The Sluice Head** — a bronze gate in the Drum's west wall that answers the Salt Hall's sluice-wheel two layers above.

**The nine.** Each is Salted at a salt-warden's strength (threat 110, [COMBAT](../systems/COMBAT.md) §1). They wake **one per world day** from the day `layer3_open` is written, in the order they sat down, and a woken one leaves the Drum and ranges — the Court first, then the Shelf by night, then the Shelf by day, which is the `salted_walking` state the baseline reaches on Thaw 40 and the reason the Salt Pans close on the 66th. A party that goes down on the first or second day meets one, which is **Even** for the level-8 party the trace's twenty hours produce (`PR` about 156 against `ER` 110) and better than Even for a party that comes back at the region's end and Overmatched for anything less; nothing in the region requires going down at all.

**What runs out.** No water above the Sump. Oil burns at a quarter in the Drum's air, so a flask that is four hours on the surface and two in the salt is thirty minutes here: a full descent and return needs six flasks and the region sells them four at a time. Bandages, at the layer-2 rate doubled. And the Sump rises a foot for every one of the nine that stands, which is the layer's clock: **the way you came in closes behind you.**

**Three returns, all stated.**
- **The Ring Stair**, until the second waking floods its lowest turn. After that it is a swim in the dark with a burden, which is the fisher trade's *Boat* verb and nobody else's.
- **The Sluice Head**, opened by a Fighter's *Break* (rating 3), a Mage's *Flood* from the Salt Hall cistern, or the sluice-wheel turned from above by a companion left there — an hour's crawl to the Salt Pans, coming up inside the Stair's boiling-sheds, which is its own problem.
- **The Names**, Read by any Drawer at any level. It is a lore reading, not a working: the preamble says what the nine were holding back and what would end it, and telling them plainly that the water above them is gone puts every woken one back on the bench for a season. `layer3_open` becomes `layer3_quiet(90 days)`, the Shelf stops being hostile by day, and the Quiet's Address opens at any tier. It is a season's reprieve and not an ending: **putting the nine down for good is the Stonereader's working and it belongs to a later region, which is why the region file states the reprieve and not the cure.**

**Cost to failure.** A death in the Court leaves the pack at the fall point ([COMBAT](../systems/COMBAT.md) §8); Wrack sentries strip it within a day unless Wrack regard ≥ +30; a companion left below is dead unless fetched within two days, and the layer's state may have changed (a hall that floods is a hall that drowns a body). A death in the Drum has no corpse-run at all, because the Sump takes the pack when the water rises: the hero wakes at Corrow's Reach owing Corrow, without the pack, and with `left_below(name)` if a companion is down there. The rumour "went into the Court and came out without them" is a weight-5 memory with everyone on the quay and a permanent line in Kit's greeting.

**Why it matters to play.** Every power's goal in the valley has a line to this place: the Stair's salvage (goal 2), the Holds' bronze (goal 2), the Quiet's seal (goal 1), the Wrack's camp and the crews they want kept out (goal 3), the Moot's fear of what walks out. Vo2 flooding the Salt Hall changes it permanently into a boat-and-breath dungeon (the fisher trade's hour). Nothing in it is required for the campaign's road: a player can leave the Court to the powers, and the season table shows what that costs.

### 7.1 The clock, built forwards

Four things move the world clock and only four ([LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §1): **open play** at one world hour per two real minutes; **map travel**, instant for the player, `distance ÷ speed` for the world; **rest and verb-days**, which advance the clock and cost the player no real time at all; and **offline**, at **four world hours per real hour away**, capped at seven world days. Dialogue, the away page, the map, the journal, shops and the confirm sheets are paused.

So a session's real length is not free: it is `open-play hours × 2 minutes`, plus the paused minutes. And an away gap's world advance is not free either: it is `real hours × 4`, exactly, with nothing to negotiate. Round one's trace broke that rule in six of seven gaps, the worst by thirty-three hours, because it was written from the dates it wanted to hit. This one is built the other way, and the table is printed so it can be checked with a calculator.

**The three checks a reader can run.** (a) Every away gap: world hours ÷ 4 = the real hours printed, exactly, and none exceeds the 168-hour cap. (b) Every session: open play + travel + rest + verb-days = the span, and open play × 2 minutes ≤ the real length. (c) The whole trace: 514 world hours in session + 484 offline (121 real hours away × 4) = **998 world hours = 41 days 14 hours**, which is Thaw 1 06:00 to Thaw 42 20:00. Twenty hours of play and 121 hours away is **141 real hours — five days and twenty-one hours**, which is the launch week of a player who plays most evenings and one long afternoon.

**What the rebuild cost.** Six of the seven old gaps were impossible and two session boundaries had to move to make the honest ones land on days the world could be met on: the Thaw 11 errand now lands at **noon**, so the hero catches Idony at the Ford Inn in her evening block instead of at her table at dawn; and Aud's and Idren's letters arrive at **dawn on Thaw 33**, not Thaw 32, so the muster and the clash are one evening apart instead of two. ### 7.3 The three ledgers

Every number in §7.2 comes from one of these three, and each is printed so it can be added up by hand. Where they and an older draft of this file disagree, these govern; where they and [LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §4 or [CLASSES_AND_STANDING](../systems/CLASSES_AND_STANDING.md) §5–6 disagree, those govern and this is the error.

**(a) Renown — heads, not deeds.** `R = Σ reach(n)` over every NPC holding a hero-memory at weight 3 or more ([CLASSES_AND_STANDING](../systems/CLASSES_AND_STANDING.md) §5). This ledger is **re-derived forwards from the deed table** ([LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §4) rather than read back off the round-two draft, which did not reproduce from the rows that govern it.

**How to re-add it with the table and a pencil.** Four rules and nothing else:

1. **Look up the row and write to everyone it names.** Every NPC at the site in that hour, the named targets at the row's weight **+2**, the targets' kin at **−2**, and then the row's own *Who takes it* column, which is where the factors, the foremen, the Speakers, the hunters and the kin lines come from. Most of the round-two errors are receivers in that column that the draft did not read to the end.
2. **A head counts once, by its strongest memory, at reach.** A second deed to the same head adds no renown. It buys time instead, and the ledger's zero rows are where the trace does its heaviest work.
3. **Decay, stated once and applied on every day it fires.** At each dawn a memory loses one weight on a clock read from the weight it is at now: at 1–5, one every ten days; at 6–9, one every thirty; at 10, never. Below 3 the head stops counting. **A new write replaces the old memory at the higher weight and restarts the clock**, so a rewritten head is not on its old schedule — which is why the ten watchmen never fall on Thaw 40, and why Drusk Fennick falls twice and comes back once. In this trace's forty-two days that means exactly one thing to watch: every memory written at weight 3, 4 or 5 has a fall due ten, twenty or thirty days later, and every one of them is a row below.
4. **Reach**: folk 1, tradesperson 2, notable 5, reeve or factor or Speaker 20, power leader 100 (§3), and the leader rule gates the last of those.

**Team and time.** Fourteen people, ten months to a first playable region on device: 2 designers (systems; content and tables), 1 writer (the tables, the anchors, the greetings), 4 engineers (simulation and engine; combat and AI; UI and platform; tools and save), 4 artists (environment; characters and enemies; UI; animation), 1 producer/QA lead with 1 QA, 1 audio (out of this document's scope but on the plan). Months 1–3: simulation and engine on a text harness. Months 3–6: combat and the parity harness (147 simulations per pass). Months 5–9: the valley's sites, people and dungeon in the build. Months 8–10: the twenty-hour trace played on device by people who did not write it, against this file.

**On the ten months, honestly.** The critic asked for a comparable and there is not one this document can cite without inventing it, so the plan is defended a different way: by a **falsifiable checkpoint at month three**. The acceptance test is that §5's baseline season reproduces in the text harness — ninety days, five planners, twenty-five schedules, the clash rolls landing where the table says — with no hand-placed events. If it does not reproduce by the end of month three, the ten months is wrong, and the stated fallback is to ship the region with **layer 3 sealed and Vo2's opening branch removed**, which costs five rooms, one encounter kind and one quest branch and nothing else, because the region is built so that layer 3 is the only piece that can be cut without cutting a system. That is a schedule risk with a named cut, which is worth more than a comparable.

**Risks named.** The engine reading as a wheel by Green (mitigated by the age rule and tier scaling; watched by a sameness count in QA). The season baseline being either too gentle or too harsh: §5 is the tuning target and the clash odds are the knob. The one-thumb fight stopping the hero on every tap (accepted; proven at Fair by the policy). **The paused share of a session:** §7.1's twenty hours are half open play and half dialogue, map, journal, shops and confirm sheets, and that is a claim this design is making rather than hiding — if the paused half is not enjoyable, the game is not enjoyable, and the first thing month-eight playtesting measures is where the thumb actually is. **Layer 3 arriving late:** it is now designed (§6) but it is the last thing built, and the named cut above is what happens if it slips. Twenty-five scheduled NPCs at 375 rows closing twenty-four hours apiece is the largest hand-authored table in the region: it is, and it is a week's work with a spreadsheet.

## Decisions this round

1. **The trace's clock is built forwards from the rule, and the away figures moved rather than the world's days.** Offline is four world hours per real hour and there is nothing to negotiate; a session's real length is its open-play hours times two minutes. Six of round one's seven gaps were impossible because the trace was written backwards from the days it wanted to reach. The rebuilt one costs two session boundaries and one world day of the toll-house's build.
2. **Map travel is offered only between sites already stood in, and never while escorting, droving, carrying or leading.** Round one's trace jumped everywhere, which made the roads free and the sessions arithmetically impossible; making first traversals and escorted moves open play is what puts the region's real minutes on its own roads.
3. **Sallowford to Wickery is eight miles in three segments of three, three and two.** Round one's four, four and two summed to eleven against the eight in two other files; the two other files were right.
4. **Meeting a reach-100 leader writes nothing; the deed in front of them does.** Corrow's hundred lands at the Salt Hall door at 22:00 on Thaw 21, four hours after the meeting at the outer stones and not at it, and a hero who takes the letter and goes home leaves with **row 67 `came_and_looked`** at weight 2 — which is a row in the table now, and not a tag this file invented. This is the region's answer to "one meeting carries a quarter of Voice", and it needed no change to the reach ladder.
5. **The reach of 100 stays, and it has a biography behind it.** Corrow was Reeve of Wickery for eleven years: sixty Wrack on the Shelf and forty in the town and on the road who were his people first. Brakka's hundred is a hall that repeats what was said before a witness. A leader who has no ear would be a notable.
6. **§4's dated power moves are the planner's printed output for the Thaw-1 state, not a script.** One dawn is worked in full so the planner can be checked against the list, each power's three ranked goals are printed because the deed table's multipliers cannot be read without them, and five of the dates are shown moving in §7 when the hero moves the state.
7. **Layer 3 of the Sunk Court is designed — five rooms, a waking clock of one Salted a day, no resources, and three stated returns including a Read that buys a season and not a cure.** Round one shipped it as a table row while the engine shipped the branch that opens it. The cure belongs to a later region and the file says so.
8. **The Court is 23 rooms, of which 11 carry an encounter entry.** That reconciles this file's count with [COMBAT](../systems/COMBAT.md) §9's without either being wrong; they were counting different objects.
9. **Every schedule closes twenty-four hours, with a stated default (home, asleep; or with the band, for people who have no home here).** Seven of twenty-five had unassigned nights, which left the meeting tick nowhere to run and a night arrival with nobody to meet.
10. **Kit Ashby unloads on the quay from 14:00 on a letter day**, which is when a letter she is carrying leaves her hand. Round one put her at Corrow's Reach at the hour both of her scenes happen on the quay.
11. **The Stand contract can be handed back at the gate, and this trace hands it back.** A rota of one day in six is income for a player who is in the world on a Stillday and a `broke_contract` at weight 7 for one who is not; an offline-safe game cannot hang a Fighter's Hand-tier income on attendance without saying so.
12. **The twenty-hour experience total is 4,370, itemised into the four categories [PROGRESSION_ECONOMY](../systems/PROGRESSION_ECONOMY.md) §1 states.** Round one's 4,343 here against 4,370 there was two unshown workings; the region's trace now adds to the economy's model rather than beside it.
13. **The renown ledger in §7.3a is re-derived forwards from the deed table, deed by deed and receiver by receiver, and the number it produces is 402 rather than round two's 395.** The round-two ledger was a reading of the table, not a derivation from it, and it was wrong in seventeen places — four the critic found and thirteen more that came out of doing the arithmetic properly. The rule stands and is now obeyed rather than asserted: where this file and the deed table disagree, the table governs. [CLASSES_AND_STANDING](../systems/CLASSES_AND_STANDING.md) §6 prints the same ledger and still prints round two's version of it; its floors and its renown rules govern, and its milestone readings — 36 on Thaw 8, 124 on Thaw 13, 405 on Thaw 34 — are superseded by §7.3a.
14. **The heading of §4 says five powers, because five powers act on the valley.** Three have a seat in it; the round-one heading counted the seats and called them the powers.
15. **Three rows were added to the deed table and the trace uses two of them.** `moved_them` (65) is what the hero does to Ondrin at the ring on Thaw 7, which round two counted at five renown with no row behind it; `came_and_looked` (67) is what meeting Halse Corrow writes; `broke_word` (66) is the mirror of `kept_word` that every "say yes and not go" branch in the engine needed. The table's count is restated honestly as **67, split 27 / 31 / 9**, and the new rows are numbered 65–67 and appended so that no citation anywhere in the bible moves.
16. **A new write restarts a head's decay clock; a head is counted once, by its strongest memory.** Both rules now sit in [LIVING_WORLD](../systems/LIVING_WORLD.md) §2 and §4, and between them they account for four of the seventeen errors: Drusk survives the 29th and falls on the 33rd and returns on the 34th, and the ten watchmen never fall at all.
17. **Three scenes moved to the hours the schedules allow, and one condition window closed by two days.** Garrow meets the drove at the Ford Inn door at 20:00, not at a forge he leaves at eighteen; the Quarter-day goes into Idren's hand at the quay at ten, where he weighs, and into Sabel's book at eleven; Kit Ashby is on the Shelf at that hour and enters the ledger on the 22nd instead, buying bronze in her own back room; and Osk is *hurt* Thaw 2–10 rather than 2–12, so that the man who hears Pell's letter read on the 11th is a man his schedule allows to be in the room. Every one of those four was carrying renown in the round-two ledger.
18. **The Thaw 39 away page's closed Salt Pans has a cause in this run.** The baseline closes them on Thaw 66 by way of `layer3_open`, which never happens here because the ring was kept — so the line now names what did happen: the Fighter broke the sluice-wheel on the 22nd and cut a dry road from the Salt Hall to the pans.
