# Combat, Dungeons and Autobattle

Real-time, portrait, one thumb. The hero moves, dodges and uses four arc abilities; two companions act on their own AI under a stance the player sets; **autobattle** hands the hero to the same AI the companions use, with the same abilities, cooldowns and stats. Field fights last 20 to 90 seconds; dungeon and set-piece fights longer. This file gives the numbers, the rules, the autobattle policy, the parity proof, death and its cost, and three encounters played twice each. Class verbs are in [CLASSES_AND_STANDING](CLASSES_AND_STANDING.md); the screen in [SESSION_UX](SESSION_UX.md); the dungeon of the first region in [FIRST_REGION](../slice/FIRST_REGION.md) §6.

---

## 1. The model

**Hero.** Health `90 + 10 × level`. Stamina 100 (Fighter, Rogue), regenerating 10 a second; Water 100 (Mage), refilled only at water. Move speed 4 m/s. **Dodge**: a flick on the movement stick; 0.4 s invulnerable, 10 stamina (or nothing for a Mage), 1.2 s cooldown.

**Weapons by tier** (tiers 1–5; region one uses 1–3): light 7 / 9 / 12 damage at one hit per 0.8 s; heavy 12 / 15 / 20 at one per 1.4 s. Mage Fling `4 + 2 × tier`; Wring `25 + 5 × tier` plus 10 a second for three seconds. **Armour** subtracts flat from every hit: cloth 0, leather 2, mail 4, hold-mail 6. Nothing crits; nothing is random except who a slinger shoots at first.

**Companions.** Health `80 + 10 × level` (Shields `100 + 10 × level`). Roles and abilities: **Shield** (Gulla, Osk): a heavy weapon, *Taunt* (every enemy within 4 m targets the Shield for 3 s; 10 s cooldown), *Brace* (block 60% for 2 s), *Crack* (a 3-second wind-up that strips a Salted thing's crust — armour to 0 — for 10 s; 12 s). **Bow** (Corva): 8 damage per 1.5 s at 10 m; *Pin* (12 damage and a 1.5 s root; 12 s); keeps 6 m from anything. **Draw** (Ilune): Fling 6, *Bind* (as the hero's), *Ease* (15 health over 5 s to the most hurt ally; 15 water; 10 s), a skin of 80. **Blade** (Kit): light weapon, Mark from behind ×2, *Powder* (as the hero's; 18 s). **Stances** (one chip per companion, tapped to cycle): *Close* (stay within 3 m of the hero), *Free* (fight by role), *Back* (disengage to the site's edge and stop fighting). Companions level with the hero and never die in field fights; in dungeons they can ([§8](#8-death-and-its-cost)).

**Ratings.** Party rating `PR = Σ (10 + 4 × level + 5 × gear tier)` over hero and companions. Encounter rating `ER = Σ threat`. A fight is **Fair** if `ER ≤ PR`, **Even** if `PR < ER ≤ 1.3 × PR`, **Overmatched** above that. The glyph on the encounter's first frame shows which (a full, a half, or a broken shield). Autobattle is built to win every Fair fight and most Even ones; a hand can do the same, and can sometimes win Overmatched ones by using what the AI does not (§6).

## 2. Ability and enemy design rules

1. Every hit over 15 is telegraphed for at least 0.6 s with a visible wind-up and a sound; every telegraph can be dodged, and each class has one arc answer to it (Hold, Bind or Mist, Slip or Snare).
2. No enemy is immune to a whole class. Immunities are to one ability, and the counter is always on the same class's arc (a husk ignores Bind; Wring kills it).
3. At most five enemies on screen in the field; set pieces run waves. Enemies have three silhouettes per kind (idle, wind-up, hurt) so the wind-up reads at thumb size.
4. Every ability has one job (damage, interrupt, block, control, escape, heal) and every class arc has damage, interrupt or control, and escape or block. No ability is strictly better than another on the arc; they answer different enemies.
5. Companions never need managing to win a Fair fight; stances are for expression (send the Bow back; keep the Shield close) and for edge cases (Back everyone out of an Overmatched hall).
6. Terrain is real: water refills a Mage and slows a hound; a doorway makes Hold a wall; a burning ledger-fire is a hazard (10 a second) to both sides.

## 3. Dungeon design rules

A dungeon is a place with a **reason** (a surfaced court, a hold's lost gallery, a counting-house cellar), **layers** (three or more, each with a state that powers have goals about — `surfaced`, `dry`, `flooded`, `sealed`), **resources that run out** (lamp oil: one flask burns four world hours and the dark is 0.5× move speed and no telegraphs visible beyond 2 m; water: for the Mage and for drinking — a party in a dry court for more than eight hours loses 5 health a world hour; bandages: 30 health over ten seconds, out of combat only) and a **cost to failure that the world remembers** (§8). World time runs inside a dungeon at the usual two real minutes per hour, so a descent is also a day the world moves without you. Each layer has one **return** (a way out that is faster than the way in once found) so a run can end on a decision, not a walk. Layers get harder by *state* not by number: a dry hall wakes Salted things; a flooded one drowns them and asks for a boat and breath. The first region's Sunk Court is in [FIRST_REGION](../slice/FIRST_REGION.md) §6.

## 4. Difficulty

There is no difficulty setting. The Fair/Even/Overmatched glyph is the difficulty, and it is honest: it is computed from the numbers above. Autobattle is the accessibility setting. A player who never touches the arc can finish the campaign on autobattle; a player who never turns it on can finish it by hand; both meet the same fights.

## 5. The autobattle policy

Autobattle runs the hero on the companion AI with a class policy. It reads only what the player can see (enemy kind, visible health bars, telegraphs, cooldowns, party health, the objective chip) and reacts to a telegraph 0.25 s after it starts — the same window a practised thumb hits. It never reads hidden state, never gets a stat, and never uses an ability the hero does not have.

**Common rules, in priority order:**
1. **Dodge** any telegraph aimed at the hero when the dodge is ready; otherwise use the class block/escape if ready.
2. **Objective chip** (set pieces): if the player has tapped an objective (a door, a fire, a person), fight within 4 m of it and never chase beyond.
3. **Heal/escape** when hero health < 35%: Mage *Mist* then *Ease*; Fighter *Rally*; Rogue *Powder* and step back; any: quick-use a bandage if out of reach of enemies for two seconds.
4. **Control** the most dangerous enemy in reach (highest threat) with the class control when it winds up a telegraph or when two or more enemies are in reach: Shove/Hold, Bind, Snare.
5. **Attack** the enemy in reach with the lowest remaining health, unless an enemy has a Rally or heal (then that one first), or the enemy is a Salted thing and the class has a ×2 (then that).
6. **Ranged first** when a slinger, crane or spitter is on screen and the hero's class can close (Slip) or reach (Fling); a Fighter lets the Bow companion take it.
7. **Retreat**: if the encounter glyph is Overmatched and party health < 50%, set all stances to *Back* and walk the hero to the site's edge; the fight ends as a flight (§8).

**Class specifics.** *Fighter:* chain Strikes so that Cleave lands when two or more are in front (it positions to keep enemies in a 90° arc); Hold on a telegraph when two are in reach, Shove when one; Rally when two allies are under 70% or the hero is under 60%. *Mage:* Fling by default; Bind the enemy targeting the hero; Mist when two or more target the hero or the Bow companion; Wring a Salted thing in reach on sight, otherwise the highest-threat living enemy when water ≥ 60; never spend below 10 water unless health < 35% (the AI will not thirst itself except to live). *Rogue:* Slip to the back of the highest-threat enemy in reach, Cut for the Mark, then lowest-health rule; Snare a rider or a charging boar on the telegraph; Powder when three or more are in reach.

**What autobattle never does** (these are the hand's edge cases): choose a kill order for reasons the numbers do not show (killing Pell first to stop his Rally *is* on the list; killing him first because he is Idony's nephew and you want him alive is not — autobattle kills, and *spare* is a hand decision); use terrain that is not marked (a doorway, a puddle, a fire); ration a resource for the next room; flee before health forces it; refuse to fight something that is hostile.

## 6. Parity proof

**Claim:** autobattle wins the fights a competent hand wins, plays the same abilities on the same hero and companions, and hands-on play earns expression and edge cases only.

**Argument.** (a) *Same instrument:* the AI uses the arc, dodge, cooldowns, stats and companion stances the player has; there is no AI-only ability, bonus or reaction faster than a practised thumb (0.25 s against a 0.6 s minimum telegraph, so both dodge every telegraph they see coming). (b) *Fair is defined by the AI:* the Fair threshold `ER ≤ PR` is tuned so that the policy above wins with the party at or above 40% health at the end, at every level, against every enemy combination in the region's encounter tables (a table of 38 field encounters and 11 dungeon rooms, each simulated with the policy; the tuning target is a 95% win rate at Fair with no companion downed). (c) *Even is a coin both can flip:* at Even the policy wins about 65%; a competent hand who follows the same priorities wins about the same; an expert hand who uses terrain and kill order can push to about 85%, and that margin is the "expression and edge cases" the brief allows. (d) *Overmatched is where hands go alone:* the policy retreats at 50% party health; a hand can choose to stay and, with terrain and rationing, sometimes win — and sometimes die, with the costs in §8. (e) *No mandatory efficiency:* nothing in the world (a quest, a door, a standing threshold, a reward) requires an Overmatched win. Every Voice quest's set piece is Even or Fair at the level the traces reach. The three encounters below show the same outcomes by hand and on autobattle within the tolerance.

## 7. Three encounters, each played twice

Times are seconds from first contact. Damage taken is after armour. The narration is the trace; the numbers are from §1.

### Encounter A — the picket at Hobb's Cross (field; Fair)

*Setting:* Thaw 8, dusk, the spoke road at Hobb's Cross. Corrow's foragers — three cutters and a slinger, back for the rest of Wat Hobb's stores — who do not know whose errand you are on. *Party:* Fighter, level 3, tier-1 heavy (12), leather (2), health 120; Corva (Bow), level 3, health 110. `PR = (10+12+5) + (10+12+5) = 54`. `ER = 12×3 + 10 = 46`: Fair.

**By hand.** 0–2 s: Corva's first arrow into the slinger (8 → 32). The hero closes on cutter 1 and Strikes (11 → 44). 2–6 s: cutters 1 and 2 arrive; Strike (C1 → 33), then the chain's Cleave catches both (C1 → 22, C2 → 44). The player has kept them in front. Incoming: two cutters, two swings each, 6 after armour: 24 → hero 96. Corva's second and third arrows: slinger 16. 6–10 s: Strike, Strike: C1 dead at 7 s. The player starts the next chain on C2 and at 9 s sees cutter 3's heavy wind-up; flicks a dodge: the 16 whistles past. Cleave hits C2 (→ 33) and C3 (→ 44). Incoming: two ordinary swings from C2: 12 → hero 84. Slinger dead at 8 s; Corva turns on C3. 10–18 s: the player Holds when C2 and C3 wind up together at 11 s (C2's heavy 16 at 20% = 3; C3's 6 blocked to 1), counters at ×1.5 (16 → C2 17), then Strikes C2 dead at 14 s. C3 (44) has taken Corva's arrows: three by 14 s (→ 20), and the hero's Cleave at 16 s (→ 9), and Corva's arrow at 17 s: dead. Incoming 10–18: 6 + 3 + 1 + 6 + 6 = 22 → hero 62. Rally at 15 s: +10 → **72**. *Outcome:* won at 17 s; hero 72/120, Corva 96/110 (two slinger stones). Deeds: `wrack_picket_broken(Hobb's Cross)`; Wat Hobb (folk, at his door) remembers.

**On autobattle.** 0–2 s: identical (Corva's AI takes the ranged first; the hero's policy closes on the nearest, C1). 2–6 s: the policy positions for a 90° arc — the same Cleave lands on C1 and C2. It Holds at 4 s because two are in reach and one wound up (a plain swing, not a heavy: the block wastes 20 stamina but saves 5) → hero 101. 6–10 s: the lowest-health rule keeps C1 (22) as target: dead at 7 s. C3's heavy at 9 s: dodged at 0.25 s. Cleave on C2 and C3. Incoming 12 → 89. 10–18 s: Hold at 11 s on the double wind-up (as the hand did, since two are in reach and a telegraph is up), counter on C2; the lowest-health rule then flips to C3 (20 after Corva's arrows) at 14 s instead of finishing C2 (17): C3 dead at 15 s, C2 at 19 s. Incoming 10–19: about 25 → 64; Rally fires at 60% (hero 72 at 13 s): +10. *Outcome:* won at **19 s; hero 74/120**, Corva 96/110. Two seconds slower (a target flip) and two health better (an extra Hold). Same class of result; the difference is expression.

### Encounter B — the Salt Hall stair, Sunk Court layer 2 (dungeon; Fair, resources)

*Setting:* Thaw 22, the second layer of the Sunk Court, dry this year. A stair down into the Salt Hall, lamp lit (one flask, two hours left). Four salt-hounds come up the stair; a Salted husk shambles behind them at 8 s. No water in the room; the hall's cistern is 40 m beyond the far door. *Party:* Mage, level 6, tier-2 (Fling 8, Wring 35), leather, health 150, water 100; Ilune (Draw), level 6, health 130, water 80; Gulla (Shield), level 6, tier-2 heavy (15), mail 4, health 160. `PR = (10+24+10) + (10+24+5) + (10+24+10) = 127`. `ER = 14×4 + 30 = 86`: Fair.

**By hand.** 0–3 s: Gulla Taunts on the stair; four hounds bite at her (4 each after mail, 0.9 s: about 18 a second) → Gulla 110 by 3 s. The player Binds the lead hound (20 water) and Flings it twice (16 each, ×2 to salt): dead at 2.5 s. Ilune Flings the second (12 each). 3–8 s: Taunt ends; two hounds turn on the hero; the player sees a lunge wind-up at 4 s and dodges; Flings: hound 2 dead at 4 s (Ilune's two and the hero's one), hound 3 at 6 s (three Flings), hound 4 at 8 s (three Flings; one bite lands: 6 → hero 144). Water: 20 + 2×9 = 38 spent → 62. Ilune Eases Gulla (→ 125). 8–14 s: the husk reaches the stair-head. The player waits — Gulla's Crack winds up three seconds and strips the crust at 11 s — then Wrings (35 ×2 = 70, plus 20 a second for three seconds): 130 ≥ 120: the husk falls at 14 s, dry, and the player's water is 62 − 30 = **32**. The husk's single swing at 10 s landed on Gulla (16 → 109). *Outcome:* won at 14 s; hero 144/150, water 32; Gulla 109/160; Ilune 130/130, water 56. The player has 32 water to cross the hall to the cistern, which is enough for one Bind on the way. Lamp: 1 h 50 m left. Deeds: `salt_hall_stair_cleared`.

**On autobattle.** 0–3 s: Gulla's AI Taunts as before. The hero's policy Binds the enemy targeting the hero (none yet — all on Gulla), so Flings the lowest-health hound (all 45: nearest): hound 1 dead at 2.5 s (three Flings; no Bind spent). 3–8 s: two hounds turn on the hero and the policy fires **Mist** (two enemies targeting the hero: 25 water) — the hand player did not, choosing a dodge instead. Under Mist the hounds lose their targets for 3 s; the hero and Ilune Fling them down: hounds dead at 5, 7 and 9 s. Water: 6 + 25 + 18 = 49 spent → 51. 9–15 s: the husk. The policy Wrings a Salted thing on sight at 9 s, *before* Gulla's Crack lands (the policy does not wait for a companion wind-up): 70 − 3 = 67 plus 60 bleed = 127 ≥ 120: dead at 12 s. Water 51 − 30 = **21**. *Outcome:* won at 12 s (faster: Mist is a good ability); hero 150/150 (untouched), water 21; Gulla 118/160; Ilune 130/130, water 68. Same result class, two seconds faster, eleven water poorer — and water is the resource this hall is about: a hand that rations reaches the cistern with a Bind to spare, and the AI reaches it with none. That is the edge case the brief permits and it costs nothing mandatory: the cistern is 40 m away and the next room is Fair without a Bind.

### Encounter C — the ledger-fire on Wickery quay (set piece; Even)

*Setting:* the season's end, the Quay Fight ([ADVENTURE_ENGINE](ADVENTURE_ENGINE.md) Vo3), in the shape it takes if Aud's ledgers are not to be read. Pell Tarn's company has reached the counting-house yard and is burning ledgers in a barrel-fire; the fire is a hazard (10 a second to anyone standing in it). Objective chip: **the ledgers** (the fire; putting it out is a 3-second Break or Flood, or a bucket for anyone). Two waves: wave 1 — two cutters, a slinger, a rider; wave 2, when wave 1 has one left or at 30 s — Pell Tarn, two cutters, a slinger, a rider. *Party:* Rogue, level 8, tier-2 light (9), leather, health 170; Kit (Blade), level 8, health 160; Gulla (Shield), level 8, tier-2, health 180. `PR = 52 × 3 = 156`. `ER = 12×4 + 10×2 + 22×2 + 45 = 157`: Even, by one point.

**By hand.** The player taps the objective chip and sets Gulla *Close*, Kit *Free*. 0–6 s: the rider charges (0.7 s wind-up): the player Snares it at 1 s — a Snared rider is dismounted, and a dismounted rider is a cutter with 70 health. Kit Powders the three in reach at 2 s. The player Slips through the slinger (i-frames through the rider's swing) and Marks from behind: 26, then Cuts: dead at 4 s. 6–18 s: Gulla Taunts the cutters at the fire's edge; the player Slips the dismounted rider (26) and works it down with Kit (dead at 11 s), then cutter 1 (dead at 15 s; Kit's Marks do most of it). Wave 2 fires at 15 s (wave 1 at one left). Incoming so far: two cutter swings on the hero (12), rider's swing on Kit (11) → hero 158, Kit 149, Gulla 168. 15–30 s: Pell's *Rally* fires as his group arrives (+20% for 6 s). The player chooses Pell first: Snare is on cooldown until 11 s (ready), lands at 16 s; Slip behind, Mark (25), Cuts at 7 each; Kit joins from the other side (Marks at 16 each). Pell's fifth-swing heavy at 21 s: the player dodges the first, is caught by the second at 26 s (28 → hero 118). Pell's 220 falls at **29 s** to the two Blades' back-Marks and Gulla's 13s. His second Rally never fires. 30–48 s: the second slinger, rider and two cutters, with the Rally gone: Powder returns at 20 s and is thrown at 31 s; Snare at 27 s stops the second rider's charge; Gulla holds the fire's edge; the last cutter dies at 47 s. Incoming 30–48: hero 24, Kit 20, Gulla 40. *Outcome:* won at 47 s; hero 94/170, Kit 129/160, Gulla 128/180; the barrel-fire put out by Gulla's Break at 33 s while the last cutters were Powdered: **one ledger of three burned**. Deeds: `counting_house = intact` (two ledgers), `pell_tarn_dead` (the player did not use the west-gate option), Wrack −50, Moot +40, Stair +20, and Garrow Tull's weight-9 memory of watching Idony's nephew go down.

**On autobattle.** The player taps the objective chip and autobattle. 0–6 s: the policy Snares the rider on its charge telegraph (rule 4: control the highest threat on a wind-up); Kit's AI Powders (three in reach); the policy takes the ranged first (rule 6): Slip, Mark, Cuts: slinger dead at 4 s. 6–18 s: identical shape; the dismounted rider dies at 12 s, cutter 1 at 16 s; wave 2 at 16 s. 16–35 s: here the policy differs. Rule 5 puts Pell first *because he has a Rally* — the same choice the hand made — but the Snare is on cooldown until 12 s and Pell is not winding up when the policy looks, so it Slips behind the nearest, which is a cutter, Marks it, and only then reaches Pell at 19 s. Pell's first Rally has fired; his second fires at 31 s because he is still alive (240 health of damage has gone into a cutter and him; he dies at **34 s**). The policy dodges both heavies (0.25 s), so the hero takes no 28s; but the rallied cutters land more: hero 170 → 122 by 35 s, Gulla 180 → 118, Kit 160 → 121. 35–56 s: Powder at 32 s, Snare on the second rider at 30 s, the last cutter dead at 55 s. The objective chip keeps Gulla within 4 m of the fire; her AI uses Break on the fire when no enemy is in reach, which happens at 38 s: **two ledgers of three burned**. *Outcome:* won at 55 s; hero 104/170, Kit 108/160, Gulla 96/180. Same result: the quay held, Pell dead, Wickery kept. Eight seconds slower, one more ledger burned (a difference the world records: the Stair's debt on one more farm is uncollectable until Reckoning — a consequence, not a loss). The hand's edge was kill order and Snare timing; the AI's edge was that it dodged both heavies. Even fight, both won; the tolerance holds.

## 8. Death and its cost

**Downed.** At 0 health the hero is down. If any companion is standing when the fight ends, the hero is carried to the nearest safe site; the world advances two days (recovery); the pack is intact; the companion who carried you gains a weight-6 memory and a regard +10; everyone at the site gains `beaten_at`. If the fight was a clash, the hero's side loses it.

**Left for dead.** If everyone is down, or the hero was alone: the hero wakes at the nearest safe site three world days later, with the coin gone (to whoever won — a Wrack band that took your purse is a state the engine can hang a quest on; Salted things leave the coin, they do not know what it is), the **pack left at the fall point** (recoverable: a corpse-run, in the Wizardry sense, and in a dungeon the layer's state may have changed by then), a **scar** (permanent −5 maximum health; at three scars the portrait shows them and the Vael greeting at *knows* adds a line; no cap: a hero who dies twenty times is a hero the world calls the Twice-Dead and the standing gains a memory in every inn, which is not nothing), and the rumour of a defeat at that site.

**Companions.** In field fights a downed companion gets up after the fight. In a dungeon, a downed companion must be carried out (Fighter at full speed, others at half) or *left*; a left companion is dead unless the hero returns to the layer within two world days and the layer's state has not changed. A dead companion is dead: their agenda ends, their kin remember (Corva's father; Ilune's keeper), the region's companion count drops, and the site where they died carries a marker. This is the cost the brief asks for and it is not softened.

**Flight.** Leaving a fight (walking the party off the site's edge, or the policy's Overmatched retreat) is a *flight*: no health cost; the site keeps its band; any escort or hold objective fails and writes its consequence; a witnessed flight is a weight-4 memory. Flight is always allowed and often right.

## 9. Sizes and risks

Encounter tables: 38 field encounters (by site type × band or creature list × time of day), 11 dungeon rooms, 3 set pieces in the first region. Fifteen enemy kinds with three silhouettes each. Twelve arc abilities, twelve knacks per class, twelve companion abilities. The tuning burden is the parity target in §6(b): every Fair encounter must be simulated on the policy at three levels and pass; that is 147 simulations per balance pass, automated. Risks: the policy's lowest-health rule can look stupid when it flips targets (mitigation: a 1.5-second target stickiness); Mage water rationing on autobattle is conservative by rule and can strand a party without a Bind (mitigation: the cistern-per-layer rule in §3); a set piece's objective chip must be discoverable in the first set piece (mitigation: the Quay Fight anchor's opening line names the ledgers and the chip pulses once).
