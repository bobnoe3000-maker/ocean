# Session and UX

A phone in one hand, held upright. This file describes the portrait screen zone by zone, the thumb map, the three session lengths and what each ends with, offline return, how the living world and the hero's standing are shown on a phone, and accessibility. The world it shows is [LIVING_WORLD](LIVING_WORLD.md); the standing it shows is [CLASSES_AND_STANDING](CLASSES_AND_STANDING.md) §5; the fights it controls are in [COMBAT](COMBAT.md).

---

## 1. The screen, zone by zone

The layout is described on a 9:19.5 portrait phone in five horizontal zones, from the top. Percentages are of screen height. The **thumb zone** is the bottom 40% and the right (or, mirrored, left) 60% of width: everything the player must touch in a fight lives there, within 70 mm of the corner the hand holds.

**Zone A — the strip (0–7%).** Left: the world clock as a glyph pair — season (a bud, a sun, a sheaf, a snowflake) and hour (a sun-arc that fills) — with the day's weather glyph. Centre: the **hearth glyph**, the hero's standing (an ember, a flame, a fire, a beacon, a hearth with the map behind it) which burns blue at a site where the hero is more resented than thanked. Right: coin, as marks and bits, and a small pack weight. Tapping the strip opens the journal (Zone E). Nothing here needs to be tapped in a fight.

**Zone B — the field (7–58%).** The world, seen from above and a little behind, the hero at the lower third so the thumb never covers what is coming. NPCs carry a name when within 6 m and a small glyph for their greeting tier (nothing, a dot, a hand, a hearth, a blue hearth). Enemies carry a health bar and, on a telegraph, a widening ring under their feet. A **target lock** appears on the nearest hostile; tapping another enemy in Zone B moves it (this is the one in-fight touch outside the thumb zone, and it is optional: the arc fires at the lock, and the lock follows the policy's priority when the player does not touch it). A pulsing **objective chip** appears here in set pieces (the ledgers, the gate) and is tapped once to set it.

**Zone C — the line (58–63%).** One line of text: the current rumour, the giver's last sentence, the quest's next step, or in a fight the encounter glyph and a two-word read of the situation ("Two behind"). Never a paragraph.

**Zone D — the thumb zone (63–100%).** The right (or left) 60%: the **arc** — four petals fanned around the corner, in a quarter-circle at 22–48 mm from it, reachable without moving the grip. The petals are the class's four arc abilities, always in the same order (basic at the bottom, then interrupt or control, block or escape, then the big one at the top). Each petal shows its cooldown as a fill and its cost as a rim. **Tap** fires at the lock; **hold and drag** aims (Fling, Snare, Powder, Shove) and releases on lift. In the arc's centre, at the corner, the **hearth button**: tap to quick-use (a bandage, a drink from the skin); **long-press** to toggle **autobattle**, which turns the arc gold and lets the player watch, or keeps aiming petals live so the player can interject one ability at any time without turning autobattle off (an interjection pre-empts the policy for that ability's duration; the policy resumes). The left (or right) 40% of Zone D and all of the lower field is the **movement surface**: touch anywhere and drag to move, the stick appearing where the thumb landed; **flick** to dodge in the flick's direction. Because the same thumb moves and taps, the hero stops for the instant of a tap; every ability is instant on tap, and the movement stick re-acquires on the next touch without a dead zone. Two **companion chips** sit at the top-left of Zone D (mirrored: top-right), each a portrait with its stance glyph; tap to cycle *Close / Free / Back*.

**Zone E — the drawer.** Swipe up from Zone C (or tap the strip) to open the drawer over the field: four tabs in a row at thumb height — **Journal** (quests by deadline, rumours by day, letters, notices copied from boards), **Map** (the one shape: the bowl, drawn as a Vael would draw it, with the first region's sites marked and the powers' marks on them — a knife for a band, a stone for a hold, a scale for a counting-house, a ring for a kept stone, a flame for a burned site, a wave for a flooded one; tap a site to travel, and the clock shows the hours it will cost), **People** (everyone the hero has a memory with, their greeting tier, their last known site, and, for companions, regard and agenda), **Pack** (gear by slot, supplies with counts, materials, and the trade's tools). The drawer is closed by a swipe down. Map travel and rest are the only actions in the drawer; everything else is reading.

**Dialogue.** A speaker's line at the bottom of the field, above the arc, with two to four answers as full-width bars in the thumb zone. Tap to answer; no timers. Long conversations are two or three exchanges; the journal keeps them.

## 2. The thumb map

Everything a fight needs is in the corner: four petals (abilities), the hearth button (quick-use; autobattle), the movement surface (move; flick to dodge), two stance chips (companions). Everything a fight does not need is out of the corner: the target lock (optional), the objective chip (once), the strip and the drawer (never in a fight). Mirroring is one setting and flips Zone D and the chips. The design rule: **a fight can be won with one thumb that never leaves the bottom-right 60 × 70 mm, and a fight can be watched with one long-press.** The hero's position in the lower third of the field keeps the thumb off the enemies.

## 3. Three sessions

Every session ends with a world change the player can name. The clock runs two real minutes per world hour in the open, pauses in menus, and jumps for travel and rest ([LIVING_WORLD](LIVING_WORLD.md) §1), so a session's world-time depends on how the player spends it.

### The three-minute session: an errand
The player opens the app. The **away page** (§4) takes fifteen seconds. The journal's top quest has a next step at one site; the player taps the map, travels (a jump of one to three world hours), and does the step: a conversation and a decision, or a field fight of 20–90 seconds, or a delivery. The step writes a deed. The player closes the app.
*Example (Thaw 11, hour 4 of play):* away page: "Two days. Rain. The Chain are asking at the Post inn who was at the bridge." Journal: *Idony's Ledger* — deliver Pell's letter to Idony. Map: Wickery → Sallowford, 3 hours; arrive Thaw 11, 10:00. Idony is at the reeve's table (schedule). Two lines, one decision (give the letter; keep it). The deed writes `pell_letter_delivered`, Idony's weight-7 memory, and the hearth glyph flickers from ember to flame — Sallowford knows the name. The line in Zone C: "Idony Sallow will not forget this." Close. *World change:* a door opened (Pell's Pardon is now possible) and a tier gained.

### The fifteen-minute session: a venture
One quest, start to end, or the middle of a bigger one to its next decision. Typically: away page; a giver's scene; travel; a field fight or a search; the decision; the return; and the consequence surfacing in a greeting or a rumour before the player closes.
*Example (Thaw 6–7, hours 2–3 of play):* *The Miller's Water* (V1). Bram at the ford; travel to the Hewers' Camp (2 hours; rain; `roads_slow`); the hewers, who will not fight over a dam but will over an insult; the player (a Fighter) breaks the dam — a 40-second scene of Break with two hewers deciding whether to stop it (they don't: a Skerrow does not fight without a contract to fight for); the river rises a little, not enough; Hild's hint about the Rudd Stones; the player climbs (3 hours) and meets Ondrin, who looses the river for a promise. Return to Fenn's Mill at dusk: the wheel is turning, and Bram says the only warm thing he says all season. *World change:* `dam = broken`, `river_flow = normal`, Fenn's Mill grinding, Holds regard −15, Quiet +10, a debt paid on Thaw 10 that would have made a Wrack recruit.

### The evening session: a descent, or a season's turn
Two to three hours: a dungeon layer, or a chain of quests to a Voice decision. The clock covers two to four world days, with rests between. The evening is where the offline cap does not apply, the engine offers its Name and Voice quests, and the map changes visibly.
*Example (Thaw 20–23, hours 7–10 of play):* *Corrow's Letter* (N1) into the Sunk Court's first layer, the salvage crew at the Salt Hall door, the choice to drive them off with Corrow; rest at Corrow's Reach (a Wrack camp that lets the hero sleep: a first); the next day the Salt Hall stair (Encounter B), the cistern, the well-ring seen; return to Wickery on Thaw 23 to a quay where Marrock will not buy the Court's bronze from you and Kit will, Kit's warning that Drusk is asking where you slept, and the Moot's notice on the board with your name in it. *World change:* `well_ring_intact` kept, Stair regard −25, Holds −10, Quiet +20, Wrack +15, the Court's first two layers on the map, and the hero's name posted in a town.

**A long evening is also rewarded** by the world's density, not by a bonus: the engine keeps offering, dungeon layers are long, and world-days keep passing so that the faction moves the player would otherwise read on an away page happen while they watch — a band arrives at the bridge as they cross it.

## 4. Offline return: the away page

On open, if more than one world hour has passed offline: one card. Top: "*N* days. *Weather.*" Then at most seven lines, chosen by the consequence system in this order — anything that touched the hero's home or party; letters arrived; map events in the hero's region by proximity; power moves the hero's regard makes personal; the season's flag changes; a rumour. Each line is a map-event template filled with names ([LIVING_WORLD](LIVING_WORLD.md) §5). Below: "Letters: 2" and "Journal: 1 deadline passed" as tappable chips. One tap dismisses; the page is kept in the journal under the day.
*Example:* "Three days. Rain, then clear, then fog tonight. — The mill at Fenn's has stopped: the Skerrow have dammed the Rudd above the Wend. — Men with knives are camped at the Rudd Bridge. — Idony Sallow asked after you twice. — A letter from Wickery: Vesk Idren, Factor. — Quarter-day interest is due in four days."

## 5. Sessions on the clock: how long is a world-day of play?

A three-minute errand spends one to four world hours. A fifteen-minute venture spends six to twelve. An evening spends two to four world days. Offline days count too, so a season of ninety days is about twenty-five hours of play for someone who opens the app once or twice a day and about forty for someone who plays in long evenings ([FIRST_REGION](../slice/FIRST_REGION.md) §7 traces the evening player: Thaw 42 at hour twenty). Either way the first region is one season, one valley, one conflict tilted.

## 6. Showing the living world and the hero's standing

- **Rumours.** Zone C's line changes at every site entry and every world hour in the open; the journal keeps the last thirty by day. Rumours are the memory system's meetings, seen from the outside: "Heard at the Post: Reyne's sold the strip above the pasture to Thrum. Someone stood witness."
- **Notice boards.** Every town and village has one; it lists the powers' *Post* moves (jobs at a standing threshold, with the hero's name on them from Name upward), the Moot's levies, the Stair's called debts (by name — this is how the player learns who will join the Wrack next), and the reeve's bans. Reading a notice copies it to the journal.
- **Letters.** Arrive at the hero's home or last inn, one to three days after a power's regard crosses a threshold or a tier changes; they are the powers' voices ([ADVENTURE_ENGINE](ADVENTURE_ENGINE.md) Vo1's two letters in one morning). The journal keeps them; the People tab shows who has written.
- **The map that changes.** Markers are site states: a dam, a knife, a scale, a flame, a wave, a stone, a ring that has gone dark (unkept). A site's control is a coloured rim (Moot green, Stair grey, Holds rust, Quiet blue, Wrack black). Tapping a marker gives the map-event line and the day it happened. The map is the same one shape at every scale — the region is a wedge of the bowl, and pinching out shows the whole bowl with the other regions greyed.
- **Greetings.** Every NPC's first line is the memory tier ([LIVING_WORLD](LIVING_WORLD.md) §2). The player never sees a number for standing; they see who says the name, who says "Morning," and who shuts the door. The hearth glyph is the only abstraction, and it has five states.
- **Prices.** Shop prices show the regard modifier as a plain word beside the price: "(neighbour's price)", "(Kest price)", "(not to you)".
- **The People tab** is the standing screen: it lists the heads that hold the hero's name and what each holds, which is exactly what renown counts.

## 7. Accessibility

One thumb, either hand (mirror). Petals and chips at a minimum of 12 mm; the movement surface is the whole lower field so there is nothing small to find. **Autobattle is the accessibility mode** and is first-class by design, so a player who cannot flick or tap fast plays the whole campaign on a long-press and the stance chips. Telegraphs are a ring, a sound and a haptic pulse, so any one channel is enough; colour is never the only signal (the powers' rims carry a glyph as well as a colour). Text scales to 150% without reflowing the arc; dialogue has no timers; Zone C can be read aloud by the system screen reader with the field paused. Reduce-motion swaps the Mist and water effects for outlines. The offline cap and the absence of daily mechanics mean there is nothing a player loses by not opening the app.

## 8. Sizes and risks

Screens: the field, the drawer's four tabs, the away page, dialogue, the store, creation, and the set-piece objective. Nine screens. The thumb-zone risk is the shared thumb: moving and tapping with one digit means the hero stops on every tap; the design accepts it (abilities are instant; enemies telegraph at 0.6 s; the dodge is a flick, not a button) and the parity policy proves a stationary tap is survivable at Fair. The legibility risk is Zone C carrying too much; the rule is one line and the journal for the rest.
