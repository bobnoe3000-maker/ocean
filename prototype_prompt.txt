# Ocean — Look Test (phase 1)

## Goal

Prove that a mobile, tilted-top-down pirate world can hit AAA quality in this
stack. Quality target: Sea of Conquest and Dredge, more top-down. Build one
scene, judge it, iterate. Nothing else.

In scope: one island port, one anchored brig, ocean, sky, lighting, post,
three times of day, one fog variant, a fixed camera with three zoom presets.
Out of scope: steering, gameplay, weather transitions, multiple islands,
audio, UI beyond a debug panel, module architecture, multi-agent fan-out.

## The bar (non-negotiable)

- Photographic PBR materials with real roughness/normal/AO maps for sand, wet
  sand, rock, weathered timber, tarred hull planks, canvas, rope, terracotta and
  lime-washed walls. Never a flat colour where a material should be.
- Physically plausible sun and sky: atmospheric scattering, a real sun disc,
  aerial perspective on distant land, correct shadow direction and colour per
  hour, a night lit by moon and lanterns rather than an ambient floor.
- A living ocean: directional swell plus wind chop, sub-surface colour in
  shallows, crest and shore foam, wet-sand shoreline, sun glitter, reflections
  broken by the surface.
- A port that reads as a place: cliffs, beach, palms that sway, dock, lanterns,
  chimney smoke, gulls, sails and flags that move.
- Never programmer art (checklist below fails a round automatically).

## Platform and stack (decided)

- Reference device: mid-range 2023 Android (Adreno 6xx) and iPhone 12. Portrait
  first. Three.js current release, WebGL2, Vite + TypeScript at repo root.
- Camera: 58° pitch, fixed yaw, three zoom presets of 60 m, 120 m and 300 m
  visible width in portrait. The hero framing is 120 m.
- Assets: glTF with meshopt or Draco, KTX2 textures, Poly Haven HDRIs. Sources:
  Poly Haven, ambientCG, Sketchfab CC0, or procedural. Log every asset in
  `assets/CREDITS.md`. If no CC0 hull is good enough, build a procedural brig.
- Budget, measured not estimated: ≤ 300 draw calls, ≤ 1.5 M triangles,
  ≤ 256 MB texture memory. Headless fps is smoke only; report device fps as
  "not measured" unless a real device was used.

## How to work

1. **LOOK.md first.** Five named reference frames (two from each game, one real
   harbour photograph at golden hour, described in words). The hero vista: island
   port at golden hour, sun 12° above the horizon, brig anchored inside the
   harbour mouth, wind 6 m/s from the port quarter. A short sheet for 12:00,
   17:30 and 22:00, and for clear versus fog. The specific material sets to use.
2. **Screenshot harness before visuals.** `tools/shoot.mjs` drives headless
   Chromium, waits for `window.__ready`, applies a JSON spec (time, weather,
   zoom, device preset), and writes PNG plus a JSON log of console errors, draw
   calls, triangles and texture memory. Device presets: portrait phone 390×844
   @3 and 1080p desktop. A contact-sheet command renders the three times by two
   weathers into one image.
3. **One builder builds the hero vista.** Then extends it to the other times of
   day and fog. No visual claim without a screenshot path.
4. **One critic**, a brutal AAA art director who writes no code, takes its own
   screenshots at all three zooms and scores 0–10 per rubric line. 10 =
   indistinguishable from the references, 8.5 = AAA with nits, 7 = good indie,
   5 = programmer art. Pass = every line ≥ 8.5 and zero console errors.
5. Below 8.5 the builder gets the ranked issue list with screenshot paths and
   goes again. Maximum 6 rounds. If still failing, stop and write down exactly
   what is missing and why.
6. Commit every round with the score in the message. Keep `docs/STATUS.json`
   current with scores, screenshot paths, open issues and measured budgets.

## Critic rubric

1. Materials: roughness variation, wear where hands and water touch, no tiling
   at any zoom, normals read under raking light.
2. Light: sun colour and shadow length match the hour; shadows soften with
   distance; bounce warms shaded sand; night is moon and lanterns.
3. Atmosphere: distant land loses contrast and shifts blue; fog has depth; the
   horizon gradient is right for the hour; bloom is restrained.
4. Water: swell follows wind; shallows show seabed; foam on crests and shore,
   not painted; reflections broken by the surface.
5. Scale and motion: ship, dock, buildings and props agree; sails, flags, palms,
   smoke and gulls move; nothing static that would move in life.
6. Composition and squint test: reads as a painting at full size and at
   thumbnail; the eye goes to the port.
7. Budget and errors: console clean; draw calls, triangles and texture memory
   within budget; fps measured or honestly "not measured".
8. Programmer-art checklist: any hit is an automatic fail.

## Programmer art (automatic fail)

Flat or untextured surfaces; default Three.js materials; visible tiling or
stretched UVs; shadow acne or missing shadows; grey nights; a uniform blue
water plane or white-stripe foam; a hard water-to-beach line; vegetation that
does not move or instances with identical rotation; fog as one colour; sky
without a sun; placeholder primitives or text labels in any screenshot; any
recognisable low-poly asset kit.

## Rules

- Never inflate scores. Real numbers, failed rounds, what is still missing.
- Do not ask me questions. Decide, record assumptions in `docs/DECISIONS.md`,
  keep going.
- Do not depend on or modify bobnoe3000-maker/emberhold.

## Done means

`LOOK.md`, `docs/STATUS.json`, `docs/DECISIONS.md` and `assets/CREDITS.md`
exist. The contact sheet (three times, two weathers) and all three zooms score
≥ 8.5 with zero console errors, or the report says exactly which lines failed
and why. Phase 2 (steering, weather, archipelago, module fan-out) is a separate
prompt and starts only after this passes.
