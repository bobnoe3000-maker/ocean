# Hearthfall — Design Test (phase 1)

## Goal

Prove on paper that a mobile, portrait, one-hero-plus-companions action RPG can
carry a world as deep as Pathfinder's and as weighty as Middle-earth, and that
the world stays alive whether or not the player is looking. Write a design
bible set, judge it, iterate. No code, no art, no engine.

In scope: an original world and its first region, the living-world systems,
real-time combat with optional autobattle, free-to-play progression and economy
without gacha, the mobile session, and a first playable region specified to the
point that a team could build it. Out of scope: engine choice, art direction,
audio, multiplayer, later regions beyond a sketch, marketing.

## The bar (non-negotiable)

- An original setting. Tolkien and Paizo shape the feel; not one name, place,
  god, race or artefact comes from either. The world has its own deep history
  (at least three ages that still matter in play), its own peoples (four to six,
  each with a way of life the player can see in a town), its own powers (gods,
  orders, crowns, guilds) with agendas that conflict.
- A living world, all four at once: people with schedules and memory; factions
  that act, expand, clash and change the map without the player; world time
  with days, weather, seasons and festivals that change who is where and what
  is possible; choices that close doors, alter regions and are remembered for
  the whole campaign.
- One hero under direct control, with companions who have their own lives,
  opinions and fights. Autobattle is a first-class way to play, never a worse
  one: it plays the same abilities on the same hero and companions, it wins the
  fights a competent hand would win, and hands-on play earns only expression and
  edge cases, never mandatory efficiency.
- Free to play with no gacha and no energy gate. Companions, power and story
  are earned in play. Money buys cosmetics, expansions and convenience that a
  patient player gets anyway. The design must say what is sold and prove the
  world stays credible with it.
- A phone in one hand, portrait, sessions of three to fifteen minutes that
  each end with something changed in the world, while a long evening of play is
  also rewarded.
- Never a design smell (checklist below fails a round automatically).

## Platform and business (decided)

- Mobile, portrait, one-hand play with a thumb zone; landscape never assumed.
- Real-time action: the hero moves, dodges and uses abilities directly;
  companions act on their own AI with orders from the player. Autobattle hands
  the hero to the same AI. Combat lasts 20 to 90 seconds in the field, longer
  only for set pieces.
- Free to play, no gacha, no stamina. Sessions can be offline-safe: the world
  simulation advances on return, summarised in a "while you were away" page.
- Tone: mythic land, Pathfinder depth. Landscape and history as characters,
  understated magic with a price, factions and systems with teeth, adventurers
  as the lens but never the centre of the world.

## How to work

Work in `rpg/` of this repository. Docs only. Use plain Markdown.

1. **PITCH.md first.** One page: the world in three sentences, the hero's
   place in it, the loop of a session, the loop of a campaign, why the world is
   alive, what is sold, the three things that make this unlike its references.
2. **The bible set.** Write these files, each standing alone and cross-linked:
   - `rpg/world/WORLD.md` — geography with a map described in words (regions,
     roads, rivers, borders and why they are where they are), history in ages
     with what each left behind, peoples, powers, magic and its price, calendar
     and seasons, the first region placed in all of this.
   - `rpg/systems/LIVING_WORLD.md` — NPC schedules and memory (what is
     remembered, for how long, how it surfaces in dialogue and price and
     access), the faction simulation (goals, resources, moves per world-day,
     how conflicts resolve, how the map changes, what the player can tilt),
     time and seasons (what a day, a week and a season change), consequence
     (how a choice is recorded, propagated and shown later). Every system has
     inputs, state, a tick, outputs and a worked example.
   - `rpg/systems/COMBAT.md` — the hero's verbs, companion roles and AI,
     ability design rules, enemy design rules, the autobattle policy in detail,
     the parity proof (what hands-on play can and cannot do that autobattle
     cannot), difficulty, death and its cost, three sample encounters played
     twice each, once by hand and once on autobattle, with the outcome.
   - `rpg/systems/PROGRESSION_ECONOMY.md` — hero and companion growth, gear,
     the sources and sinks of every currency, the fairness model (what a free
     player has after 1, 10, 50 and 200 hours; what a paying player has), what
     is sold and why the world survives it, the anti-grind rules.
   - `rpg/systems/SESSION_UX.md` — the portrait screen described zone by zone,
     the thumb map, the three-minute session, the fifteen-minute session, the
     evening session, offline return, how the living world is shown (rumours,
     notice boards, letters, a map that changes), accessibility.
   - `rpg/slice/FIRST_REGION.md` — the first region as a buildable proof: map
     in words, the town and its twenty named people with schedules, three
     factions present and their opening moves, one season of world events,
     three quest chains with consequences that cross, one day of play narrated
     hour by hour showing every system firing.
3. **No claim without a trace.** A design claim ("factions act without the
   player") is backed by a worked example in the document: state before, tick,
   state after, what the player sees.
4. **One designer writes.** One critic, a brutal AAA design director who writes
   no design, reads every file and judges by playing it on paper: three
   narrated sessions (three minutes, fifteen minutes, one evening) traced
   strictly from what the documents specify. Where the trace has to invent
   something the documents do not say, that is a hole and is listed.
5. The critic scores 0–10 per rubric line. 10 = the world reads as deep as
   Golarion and as weighty as Middle-earth and every system is specified to
   build; 8.5 = ship-ready design with nits; 7 = a good indie design doc;
   5 = a feature list. Pass = every line ≥ 8.5 and no design-smell hit.
6. Below 8.5 the designer gets the ranked issue list with file paths and
   section names and goes again. Maximum 6 rounds. If still failing, stop and
   write down exactly what is missing and why.
7. Commit every round with the scores in the message. Keep
   `rpg/docs/STATUS.json` current with scores, open issues and word counts.
   Record every assumption in `rpg/docs/DECISIONS.md`. Critic reports go to
   `rpg/docs/critic/roundN.md` and `roundN.json`.

## Critic rubric

1. World: original, coherent, deep; history that still matters in play;
   peoples and powers that could sustain a decade of stories; a map that makes
   geographic and political sense.
2. Living world: all four minimums specified with inputs, state, tick, outputs
   and worked examples; the traces show the world changing without the player
   and remembering the player.
3. Combat and autobattle: readable one-thumb action; companions worth having;
   autobattle at parity; the six sample-encounter outcomes are credible and
   consistent with the rules.
4. Progression and economy: the fairness model holds at every hour mark;
   nothing sold buys power or time; sources and sinks balance on paper; no
   grind wall.
5. Session and UX: the three session lengths each end with a world change; the
   portrait screen works one-handed; the living world is visible on the phone,
   not only in the simulation.
6. Narrative and consequence: quests cross, close doors and are remembered;
   the day-of-play trace fires every system at least once; the hero is a lens,
   not the axis of the world.
7. Feasibility and honesty: content volumes counted; the first region buildable
   by a stated team size in a stated time; risks named; nothing promised
   that the documents do not specify.
8. Design-smell checklist: any hit is an automatic fail.

## Design smells (automatic fail)

Gacha or energy under another name (timers, keys, pulls, pity); anything sold
that buys power or skips the world; a chosen-one hero with no cost or peer;
factions that only react to the player; lore that never touches play; a system
with no decision in it; an autobattle that is strictly better or strictly worse
than play; a feature listed without a session example; placeholder names
("Kingdom A", "Faction 2"); numbers without a model behind them; a screen
that needs two hands or landscape; any name, place, god, race or artefact
recognisable from Tolkien, Paizo or any other IP; "TBD" anywhere in the bible.

## Done means

`rpg/PITCH.md`, the five system and world files, `rpg/slice/FIRST_REGION.md`,
`rpg/docs/STATUS.json`, `rpg/docs/DECISIONS.md` and the critic reports exist.
Every rubric line scores ≥ 8.5 with no design-smell hit, or the report says
exactly which lines failed and why. Phase 2 (art direction, engine, first
playable) is a separate prompt and starts only after this passes.
